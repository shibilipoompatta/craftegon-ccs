COMMON INQUIRIES

Frequently Asked Compliance Questions

Straightforward answers to the most common questions founders, directors, and finance heads ask about incorporating and staying compliant in India.

How fast can my Private Limited company be incorporated in India? +

Under the MCA V3 portal and the unified SPICe+ (INC-32) filing mechanism, company incorporation typically completes in 3 to 5 business days once DSCs and identity documents are verified. This package includes Name Reservation (Part A), SPICe+ Part B, 2 Director DINs, Certificate of Incorporation (CoI), PAN, TAN, EPFO, ESIC, and zero-balance corporate bank account setup.

What are the mandatory annual RoC secretarial compliance deadlines? +

Key company compliance items include Form AOC-4 (financial statements) within 30 days of the AGM and Form MGT-7/7A (annual return) within 60 days of the AGM, subject to the applicable rules and company category. DIR-3 KYC is a separate requirement: under amended Rule 12A, it is generally due once in every third consecutive financial year by 30 June following that cycle; specified changes to mobile number, email address or residential address must be intimated within 30 days. Section 403 additional fees concern delayed filings under Sections 92 and 137; they should not be applied as a generic DIR-3 KYC penalty. Confirm current MCA requirements for each case.

Are government MCA portal fees and stamp duties included in packages? +

Before engagement, the customer should receive an itemised quotation identifying the professional fee, applicable statutory charges, stamp duty, taxes, inclusions and exclusions. Government fees and stamp duty vary by filing, entity, capital and state; confirm the applicable amount against the current official MCA / RoC challan.

Can foreign founders or NRIs incorporate an entity in India? +

Yes. Foreign nationals, NRIs, and foreign corporate bodies can incorporate wholly-owned subsidiaries in India under the 100% Automatic FDI Route across most industry sectors. At least one director on the board must be a resident of India (present in India for at least 182 days in the preceding financial year). Foreign KYC documents require apostille or consular notarization in the origin country.

In strict compliance with the Advocates Act, 1961 and Bar Council regulations, CCS does not provide contentious courtroom representation. Formal tribunal or High Court litigation representation is referred to independent practicing advocates engaged under a separate vakalatnama.
