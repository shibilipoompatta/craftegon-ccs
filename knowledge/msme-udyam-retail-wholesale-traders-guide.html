Retail & Wholesale Trade Priority Sector Lending (PSL) NIC Codes 45, 46 & 47

Udyam Registration for Retail & Wholesale Traders: PSL Benefits vs Chapter V Exclusions

Author: Banking & Regulatory Compliance Desk Published: 16 November 2026 Status: Active Policy Guidelines Reading Time: 11 mins
TRADER SCOPE

The Crucial Regulatory Bifurcation for Traders

While Retail and Wholesale Traders (NIC Divisions 45, 46, and 47) are legally permitted to obtain Udyam Registration, their registration benefits are statutorily restricted solely to Priority Sector Lending (PSL) from commercial banks and CGTMSE credit guarantees. Under explicit Ministry of MSME directives, traders are strictly excluded from Chapter V delayed payment protections (MSME Samadhaan / MSEFC arbitration), and buyers from traders are not subject to Section 43B(h) / Section 37(2)(g) expense disallowances.

1. Policy Evolution: The July 2021 Office Memorandum

When the MSMED Act was enacted in 2006 and subsequently when Udyam was notified in June 2020, entities engaged in wholesale and retail trade were completely ineligible for registration. The definition of enterprise under Section 2(e) was interpreted as requiring manufacturing or the rendering of distinct services, whereas trading was viewed as pure distribution.

Following extensive economic representations during post-pandemic recovery, the Ministry of MSME issued Office Memorandum No. 5/2(2)/2021-E/P & G/Policy dated 2 July 2021, followed by operational clarifications on 1 September 2021. This circular officially permitted registration on the Udyam portal for three major National Industrial Classification (NIC-2008) divisions:

  • NIC Code 45: Wholesale and retail trade and repair of motor vehicles and motorcycles.
  • NIC Code 46: Wholesale trade, except of motor vehicles and motorcycles.
  • NIC Code 47: Retail trade, except of motor vehicles and motorcycles.

2. The Comprehensive Trader Benefits Matrix

Entrepreneurs and commercial buyers must understand the exact statutory perimeter governing registered traders:

Statutorily Available to Traders:

  • Priority Sector Lending (PSL): Access to bank credit classified under RBI PSL targets, leading to competitive interest rates.
  • CGTMSE Coverage: Eligibility for collateral-free credit guarantee facilities up to ₹5 Crore from participating financial institutions.
  • TReDS Platform Access: Discounting of trade bills via RBI-authorized Trade Receivables Discounting System platforms.
  • Trademark Government Fee Concession: 50% discount on official filing fees for Form TM-A with the Trade Marks Registry.

Statutorily Excluded for Traders:

  • No MSME Samadhaan / MSEFC Filing: Traders cannot file recovery petitions under Section 18 for delayed payments.
  • No 20.25% Section 16 Interest: Statutory 3x RBI bank rate compound interest does not apply to trade receivable disputes.
  • No Section 43B(h) / Section 37(2)(g) Disallowance: Buyers from traders do not face corporate tax expense add-backs if invoices are delayed.
  • No GeM Public Procurement Quotas: Price preferences and tender exemptions reserved for manufacturing MSEs do not extend to pure resellers.

3. Legal & Judicial Rationale: Why Traders Are Excluded

The deliberate exclusion of traders from Chapter V and tax disallowances is rooted in the legislative text of the MSMED Act, 2006. Section 2(e) defines an "enterprise" as an undertaking engaged in the manufacture or production of goods or engaged in providing or rendering of services. The 2 July 2021 OM was issued as an executive policy measure specifically to enable RBI credit classification under Section 10 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, not as a statutory amendment to Section 2(e) or Chapter V of the MSMED Act.

Multiple High Courts (including the Bombay High Court and Delhi High Court) have consistently ruled that administrative circulars cannot rewrite statutory definitions. Consequently, MSE Facilitation Councils routinely reject applications filed by entities whose registered activities are confined to NIC codes 45, 46, and 47.

4. Hybrid Entities: Combining Trade with Manufacturing or Services

A frequent corporate structuring question arises when an enterprise conducts both trading and manufacturing/service activities (e.g. assembling hardware while reselling third-party components).

Corporate Structuring Rules for Hybrid MSMEs:

  • Activity-Specific Protection: Delayed payment protections and Section 43B(h) / Section 37(2)(g) deductions apply only to supplies originating from manufacturing or service activities. An invoice issued purely for traded goods will not qualify, even if the supplier's Udyam certificate lists manufacturing codes.
  • Segregated Invoicing: Hybrid enterprises should maintain distinct billing series and clear activity descriptors on invoices to withstand tax audit scrutiny.
  • Udyam Activity Mapping: CraftEgon CCS advisors assist hybrid enterprises in properly structuring their primary and secondary NIC codes on the official portal to ensure full compliance.
