MSME Delayed Payment Protection: Chapter V, 20.25% Interest, Samadhaan & MSEFC Handbook

Author: Corporate Law & Dispute Avoidance Group Published: 16 November 2026 Status: Active Statutory Framework Reading Time: 15 mins
🛡️

The Overriding Legal Power of Chapter V (Section 24)

Chapter V of the MSMED Act, 2006 (Sections 15 through 24) carries a powerful non-obstante clause under Section 24: its provisions have effect notwithstanding anything inconsistent therewith contained in any other law for the time being in force or in any contract, purchase order, or agreement. Buyers cannot contract out of Chapter V: clauses attempting to waive statutory interest or establish 90-day payment cycles are null and void under Indian law.

1. Legislative Architecture: Sections 15 to 24 of the MSMED Act, 2006

Enacted to eliminate severe commercial imbalances where small vendors were forced to function as uncompensated financiers to large corporations, Chapter V constructs a complete, self-contained statutory code for commercial receivable enforcement:

Section Statutory Scope Key Legal Mandate
Section 15 Duty of Buyer to Pay Mandatory payment within 15 days (default) or agreed period up to a hard ceiling of 45 days.
Section 16 Statutory Penal Interest Compound interest with monthly rests at 3 times the RBI Bank Rate (currently 20.25% p.a.).
Section 17 Recovery of Amount Due Buyer is liable to pay the invoice amount alongside accrued compound interest.
Section 18 Reference to MSEFC Statutory dispute mechanism: conciliation and arbitration before State Facilitation Councils within 90 days.
Section 19 Mandatory 75% Pre-Deposit No court or appellate body can entertain a challenge against an MSEFC award without a 75% cash deposit.
Section 22 Financial Statement Disclosure Buyer corporate audit reporting: unpaid MSE balances and interest must be published in balance sheets.
Section 23 Tax Disallowance of Interest Interest paid or payable under Section 16 cannot be deducted as an expense for income tax computation.

2. Section 16 Penal Interest: 3x RBI Bank Rate with Monthly Rests

Under Section 16, interest liability is statutory, automatic, and begins accruing from the day immediately following the appointed day or agreed due date.

The Compounding Computational Formula:

Interest is calculated at three times the Bank Rate notified by the Reserve Bank of India under Section 49 of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, compounded with monthly rests:

Applicable Rate = 3 × Current RBI Bank Rate
With RBI Bank Rate at 6.75% → Statutory Rate = 3 × 6.75% = 20.25% per annum
Compounding Frequency: Monthly Rests (Unpaid interest capitalized at end of each 30-day cycle)

Section 23 Tax Double Whammy: Not only must the buyer pay 20.25% p.a. compounding interest, but Section 23 of the MSMED Act expressly disallows this interest expenditure as a tax deduction under the Income-tax Act. The buyer must pay this interest out of post-tax profits.

3. The MSME Samadhaan Portal: Digital Application Procedure

The Ministry of MSME launched the MSME Samadhaan Portal (samadhaan.msme.gov.in) to streamline Section 18 references. The filing follows a standardized e-procedural path:

PHASE 1 • VERIFICATION
Udyam & Aadhaar Authentication

The enterprise logs into the Samadhaan portal using its active Udyam Registration Number (URN) and Aadhaar OTP verification of the authorized signatory.

PHASE 2 • EVIDENCE
Upload of Commercial Proof

Upload scanned copies of Purchase Orders (or service agreements), Tax Invoices, Delivery Challans / Proof of Delivery, and prior payment reminder correspondence (PDF up to 2MB).

PHASE 3 • INTIMATION
Automated System Notice to Buyer

Upon submission, the portal generates an official application acknowledgement and dispatches an automated legal intimation to the buyer's registered email and corporate PAN profile.

PHASE 4 • MSEFC ADJUDICATION
Council Conciliation & Award

The application is forwarded to the territorial Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council (MSEFC) for mandatory conciliation and arbitral award under Section 18.

4. Section 18 MSEFC Proceedings: Statutory Conciliation & Arbitration

Under Section 18, the MSEFC functions under statutory timelines and procedural powers imported from the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996:

  • Mandatory Conciliation (Section 18(2)): The Council first initiates conciliation under Sections 65 to 81 of the 1996 Act. The parties are given opportunity to reach an amicable settlement.
  • Automatic Arbitration (Section 18(3)): If conciliation is unsuccessful or rejected by either party, the Council either conducts arbitration proceedings itself or refers the dispute to any alternative dispute resolution institution.
  • 90-Day Statutory Deadline (Section 18(5)): Every reference made under Section 18 must be decided within a statutory period of ninety (90) days from the date of making such reference.
  • Enforceability of Award: An arbitral award delivered by the MSEFC carries the exact legal status of a Civil Court decree and can be executed under Order XXI of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908.

5. The Section 19 Shield: Mandatory 75% Court Pre-Deposit

Under general civil law, judgment debtors frequently file Section 34 appeals to stay arbitral awards, tying up claimants for years. The MSMED Act purposefully closes this loophole through Section 19:

Statutory Language of Section 19:

"No application for setting aside any decree, award or other order made either by the Council itself or by any institution or centre providing alternate dispute resolution services to which a reference is made by the Council, shall be entertained by any court unless the appellant (not being a supplier) has deposited with it seventy-five per cent of the amount in terms of the decree, award or, as the case may be, the other order..."

The Supreme Court of India in landmark decisions (including Gujarat State Civil Supplies Corporation Ltd. v. Mahakali Foods Pvt. Ltd.) affirmed that Section 19 is mandatory and jurisdictional. No District Court, Commercial Court, or High Court can entertain an appeal or grant a stay on an MSEFC award without the buyer first depositing 75% of the total awarded principal plus compound interest. Furthermore, the court has discretionary power to release a reasonable portion of this deposit to the MSME supplier pending final disposal.

6. Corporate Transparency: Section 22 Disclosures & ROC Form MSME-1

In addition to taxation disallowances, non-compliant buyers face mandatory disclosure obligations enforced under Indian company law:

  • Section 22 Annual Balance Sheet Disclosures: Every buyer company must disclose in its annual financial statements the principal amount and interest due remaining unpaid to any supplier as at the end of each accounting year.
  • Half-Yearly ROC Form MSME-1: Under Section 405(1) of the Companies Act, 2013 and the Specified Companies (Furnishing of information about payment to micro and small enterprise suppliers) Order, 2019, all companies with outstanding payments to MSEs exceeding 45 days must file Form MSME-1 half-yearly with the Registrar of Companies (RoC).
  • Penal Consequences: Failure to file Form MSME-1 attracts penalty under Section 405(4) of the Companies Act on the company and every officer in default.
