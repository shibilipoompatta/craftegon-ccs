GSTR-9C Reconciliation Statement Handbook: Self-Certification & Audit Alignment

Complete operational and statutory handbook for Form GSTR-9C under Section 44(2) and Rule 80(3). Explains the ₹5 Crore turnover threshold, PAN-level aggregation, the shift to self-certification, turnover reconciliation, and expense-wise ITC auditing.

Author: Indirect Taxation & Corporate Audit Desk • Authority: CBIC / GSTN (Act No. 12 of 2017) • Updated: FY 2024-25 & FY 2025-26 Mandates

1. Statutory Mandate & The Evolution of Self-Certification

Form GSTR-9C is the statutory reconciliation statement governed by Section 44(2) of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 80(3) of the CGST Rules, 2017. Its purpose is to reconcile the gross turnover, taxable turnover, tax liability, and Input Tax Credit (ITC) declared in the taxpayer's annual return (Form GSTR-9) against the figures audited in the entity's annual financial statements.

Historical Legislative Transition: Finance Act, 2021

Originally, Section 35(5) of the CGST Act mandated every taxpayer with turnover exceeding ₹2 Crores to have their accounts audited by a practicing Chartered Accountant (CA) or Cost Accountant (CMA) who certified Part B of Form GSTR-9C. The Finance Act, 2021 repealed Section 35(5) and amended Section 44, replacing the mandatory CA/CMA certification with a taxpayer self-certified reconciliation statement for taxpayers whose aggregate turnover exceeds ₹5 Crores.

2. Aggregate Turnover Calculation & Multi-GSTIN Entities

The statutory threshold of ₹5 Crores for Form GSTR-9C is not determined per GST registration or State branch; it is determined on an All-India PAN basis under Section 2(6) of the CGST Act.

Turnover Components Included Under Section 2(6):

  • All taxable supplies of goods and services made within the state and inter-state.
  • Exempt supplies, nil-rated supplies, and non-GST supplies (e.g. electricity, petroleum).
  • Exports of goods or services or both (zero-rated supplies).
  • Inter-State supplies between distinct persons (branch transfers) possessing the same PAN.

Explicit Exclusions:

  • Central Tax (CGST), State Tax (SGST), Integrated Tax (IGST), Union Territory Tax (UTGST), and GST Compensation Cess.
  • The value of inward supplies on which tax is payable by the recipient under reverse charge (RCM).
Multi-GSTIN Multiplier Effect

If an enterprise with an all-India PAN aggregate turnover of ₹6 Crores operates branches in 3 different states (e.g., Kerala ₹2 Cr, Karnataka ₹2 Cr, Tamil Nadu ₹2 Cr), all three registrations must independently file Form GSTR-9C, even though the turnover of each individual state registration is below ₹5 Crores.

3. Architectural Table-by-Table Breakdown of Form GSTR-9C

Form GSTR-9C is structured into Part A (Reconciliation Statement) and Part B (Self-Certification Verification).

Part I: Basic Details

Captures the Financial Year, GSTIN, Legal Name, Trade Name, and declaration regarding audited financials.

Part II: Reconciliation of Gross Turnover (Tables 5 through 8)

Table 5 establishes the reconciliation between the gross turnover reported in the audited annual financial statements and the total turnover declared in Form GSTR-9 (Table 5N).

  • Table 5A: Turnover as per audited financial statements for the State / UT.
  • Table 5B: Unbilled revenue at the beginning of the Financial Year (+).
  • Table 5C: Unadjusted advances at the end of the Financial Year (+).
  • Table 5D: Deemed supplies under Schedule I without consideration (+).
  • Table 5E: Credit notes issued post financial year end but accounted in books (+/-).
  • Table 5F: Trade discounts accounted in books but not permissible under GST Section 15(3) (+).
  • Table 5G: Turnover for the period under composition scheme (-).
  • Table 5H to 5O: Adjustments for foreign exchange fluctuations, adjustments on account of supply of goods by SEZ units, and other statutory reconciliations.
  • Table 5P: Annual turnover declared in GSTR-9 Table 5N.
  • Table 5Q: Unreconciled turnover difference (5R = 5A adjusted - 5P).
  • Table 6: Reasons for Unreconciled difference in Annual Gross Turnover.
  • Table 7 & 8: Reconciliation of Taxable Turnover (deducting exempt, zero-rated, and reverse charge supplies).

Part III: Reconciliation of Tax Paid (Tables 9 to 11)

  • Table 9: Rate-wise reconciliation of taxable value and tax liability (5%, 12%, 18%, 28%, 3%, 0.25%, and RCM). Computes total tax payable.
  • Table 10: Reasons for unreconciled payment of tax between GSTR-9 Table 9 and GSTR-9C Table 9.
  • Table 11: Additional tax liability payable due to non-reconciliation across tax heads.

Part IV: Reconciliation of Input Tax Credit (Tables 12 to 16)

  • Table 12A: ITC availed as per audited annual financial statements for the State/UT.
  • Table 12B: ITC booked in earlier financial years claimed in current financial year (+).
  • Table 12C: ITC booked in current financial year to be claimed in subsequent financial years (-).
  • Table 12D: Adjusted ITC as per books of accounts.
  • Table 12E: Net ITC availed as per Form GSTR-9 (Table 7J).
  • Table 12F: Unreconciled ITC difference (12D - 12E).
  • Table 13: Reasons for unreconciled difference in ITC.
  • Table 14: Expense-wise reconciliation of ITC (freight, legal charges, rent, repairs): optional for recent financial years but highly recommended during internal audit.

Part V: Management Disclosures & Additional Liability

Highlights additional tax liabilities arising from unreconciled turnover, rate differences, ineligible ITC, or RCM omissions, with instruction to discharge liability through Form DRC-03 in cash.

Part B: Formal Self-Certification Verification

The Authorized Signatory signs a formal legal verification under digital signature confirming that the reconciliation statement is true and correct, and that the financial records reflect the true state of affairs.

4. Circular No. 246/03/2025-GST: GSTR-9 & 9C Interdependence

Official Circular Mandate: Section 47 Cumulative Late Fee

Under Circular No. 246/03/2025-GST dated 30 January 2025, the CBIC clarified that for taxpayers with aggregate turnover exceeding ₹5 Crores, Form GSTR-9 and Form GSTR-9C constitute a single integrated annual return obligation. Filing GSTR-9 before the December 31 deadline while delaying GSTR-9C does NOT stop the Section 47 late fee clock. Late fees continue to accumulate until both Form GSTR-9 and Form GSTR-9C are successfully transmitted.
