Statutory Legal Reality: Why Notarisation is NOT RoF Registration

A widespread misconception in Indian business is believing that executing a partnership deed on stamp paper and having it notarized by a Notary Public completes registration. This is legally false. Notarisation under the Notaries Act, 1952 merely verifies signatory identity under Section 85 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023.

An unregistered notarized firm remains subject to the complete civil disabilities of Section 69 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932: you CANNOT file a civil suit in any court against trade debtors or third parties to enforce contracts, nor can co-partners sue each other. To enjoy full legal standing, the firm must be registered in the Register of Firms under Section 59.

Frequently Asked Legal & Procedural Questions

Can a partnership firm be registered after it has already commenced business?

Yes. Under Section 58 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, a partnership firm may be registered at any time during the continuance of the partnership. However, an unregistered firm cannot enforce any commercial contracts in court until it is formally registered. Subsequent registration cannot cure a defect in an already instituted lawsuit (Shiv Developers v. Aksharay Developers, 2022 SC).

Can a minor be a partner in a partnership firm?

Under Section 30, a minor cannot be a full partner because a minor cannot enter into a valid contract (Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose). However, with the unanimous consent of all partners, a minor may be admitted to the benefits of partnership. The minor is not personally liable; only their share in the firm is liable. Upon attaining majority, the minor must elect within 6 months whether to become a full partner, failing which they automatically become a partner with retrospective personal liability.

What is the difference between an unregistered firm and a registered firm regarding tax filings?

For income tax purposes, a firm is assessed as a partnership firm (PF) under Section 184 provided a certified copy of the written partnership deed is submitted with the first return. RoF registration is not strictly mandatory for income tax assessment. However, RoF registration is vital for civil legal protection and court enforceability under Section 69.

Can a partnership firm be converted into a Private Limited Company or LLP later?

Yes. A registered partnership firm can be converted into an LLP under Section 55 and the Second Schedule of the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008, or into a Private Limited Company under Part I of Chapter XXI (Sections 366 to 374) of the Companies Act, 2013, with complete tax neutrality on capital gains under Section 47(xiii) subject to statutory conditions.
