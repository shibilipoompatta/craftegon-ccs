The One Person Company Concept & Corporate Structure

Under Section 2(62) of the Companies Act, 2013, an OPC is defined as a company which has only one person as a member. By virtue of Section 3(1)(c), an OPC is legally classified as a Private Limited Company, endowed with perpetual succession, separate legal personality, and limited liability under Section 9.

Critical Distinction: Sole Member vs. Board of Directors

  • Sole Shareholder (Member): An OPC can have only 1 shareholder (holding 100% of share capital). Admitting a second shareholder requires statutory conversion into a multi-member private company.
  • Board of Directors: While an OPC must have a minimum of 1 director (who is typically the sole member), under Section 149(1) it may appoint up to 15 directors. A founder can retain 100% equity while delegating operational management to professional co-directors.
  • Limited Liability Shield: The sole member's personal liability is strictly limited to the unpaid amount of their subscribed share capital. Personal assets (bank accounts, real estate) remain legally insulated from business creditor claims.

Statutory Eligibility & Restrictions under Rule 3

Rule 3 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014 governs the eligibility of members and nominees, as substantially liberalized by the 2021 Second Amendment:

Indian Citizens (Resident & NRI)

Only a natural person who is an Indian citizen (whether resident in India or otherwise) can incorporate an OPC or act as a nominee. Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) who hold Indian passports are fully eligible.

120-Day Residency Benchmark

The stay threshold for Indian resident status was reduced from 182 days down to not less than 120 days during the immediately preceding financial year.

One Person, One OPC Limit

A natural person cannot incorporate more than one OPC or act as a nominee in more than one OPC at any time (Rule 3(2)).

Mandatory Statutory Bars & Prohibitions:

  • Foreign Nationals: Strictly barred from incorporating an OPC or acting as a nominee (regardless of domestic residency).
  • Minors (Rule 3(4)): A minor cannot become a member or nominee, nor hold shares with beneficial interest.
  • Body Corporates / LLPs: Only natural living persons are eligible; companies or LLPs cannot be members.
  • Charitable Objects (Rule 3(5)): An OPC cannot be incorporated as or converted into a Section 8 non-profit company.
  • Investment / NBFC Ban (Rule 3(6)): An OPC cannot carry out non-banking financial investment activities, including investment in securities of any bodies corporate.

Nominee Appointment & The 2023 INC-3 Procedural Clarification

Under the first proviso to Section 3(1) and Rule 4 of Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014, the sole subscriber must designate a Nominee in the Memorandum of Association who shall, in the event of the member's death or incapacity to contract, become the member of the company.

Mandatory Procedural Alert: How Nominee Consent is Captured in MCA V3 (2023 Amendment)

No Standalone Form INC-3 at Initial Incorporation: Under the Companies (Incorporation) Amendment Rules, 2023 (w.e.f. 23.01.2023), the historical procedure of executing a separate standalone e-Form INC-3 was superseded. Nominee identity, address proofs, demographic particulars, and consent declaration are now directly captured within the integrated SPICe+ (INC-32) Part B webform. Do not file a separate INC-3 e-form at incorporation.

Subsequent Change or Replacement of Nominee (Form INC-4):

Whenever the nominee is changed or withdraws post-incorporation, the company must file Form INC-4 with the Registrar of Companies:

  • Change of Nominee by Member (Rule 4(4)): The member can change the nominee at any time by giving written notice to the company. The company must file Form INC-4 within 30 days of receiving notice.
  • Withdrawal of Consent by Nominee (Rule 4(3)): If a nominee withdraws consent, the member must nominate a replacement within 15 days, and the company must file Form INC-4 within 30 days.
  • No MOA Alteration: Under the fourth proviso to Section 3(1), changing a nominee shall not be deemed to be an alteration of the Memorandum, avoiding special resolutions and stamp duties.

Frequently Asked Questions (OPC FAQs)

Can a Non-Resident Indian (NRI) incorporate an OPC in India?

Yes. Under the amended Rule 3 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014 (effective April 1, 2021), any natural person who is an Indian citizen, whether resident in India or otherwise (including NRIs holding Indian passports), is legally eligible to incorporate an OPC and act as a nominee.

Can a foreign national or foreign company incorporate an OPC?

No. Foreign nationals, foreign citizens, and overseas corporate bodies are strictly prohibited from incorporating an OPC or acting as a nominee. A foreign national or entity wanting to establish a wholly-owned subsidiary in India must incorporate a multi-member Private Limited Company.

Is Form INC-3 still filed as a separate e-form at incorporation?

No. Under the Companies (Incorporation) Amendment Rules, 2023, nominee consent is integrated directly into the SPICe+ (INC-32) Part B webform. However, for any subsequent change or replacement of a nominee post-incorporation, the company must file Form INC-4 within 30 days.

Is an OPC required to hold Annual General Meetings (AGMs)?

No. Under the proviso to Section 96(1) of the Companies Act, 2013, One Person Companies are completely exempt from the requirement to hold Annual General Meetings (AGMs). Any resolution is passed simply by entering it into the minutes book and having it signed and dated by the sole member under Section 122.

Can an OPC be voluntarily converted into a Private Limited Company?

Yes. Under amended Rule 6 (effective April 1, 2021), the historical 2-year waiting bar and ₹50 Lakh capital / ₹2 Crore turnover caps were abolished. An OPC can voluntarily convert into a Private Limited Company or Public Company at any time by increasing its members and directors and filing Form INC-6.
