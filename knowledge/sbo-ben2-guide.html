Significant Beneficial Ownership (SBO) & Form BEN-2 Guide

A forensic regulatory breakdown of Section 90 of the Companies Act, 2013, calculating indirect natural person ownership, issuing statutory BEN-4 inquiries, maintaining the BEN-3 register, and avoiding RoC adjudication penalties.

1. Who Qualifies as a Significant Beneficial Owner (SBO)?

Under Section 90 of the Companies Act, 2013 read with the Companies (Significant Beneficial Owners) Rules, 2018, a Significant Beneficial Owner (SBO) is an individual (natural person) who, acting alone or together through one or more persons or trusts, possesses one or more of the following rights or entitlements in a reporting company:

  • Holds indirectly, or together with any direct holdings, not less than 10% of the shares.
  • Holds indirectly, or together with any direct holdings, not less than 10% of the total voting rights.
  • Has the right to receive or participate in not less than 10% of the total distributable dividend or any other distribution in a financial year through indirect holdings.
  • Has the right to exercise, or actually exercises, significant influence or control (other than through direct holdings) over the reporting company.
The Golden Rule of SBO: An individual holding shares strictly directly in their personal name is NOT an SBO. SBO provisions are triggered only when an individual holds shares through an intermediary legal vehicle (e.g., holding company, LLP, trust, or partnership entity).

2. The 4 Intermediary Ownership Tests

To determine the natural person SBO through corporate holding chains, companies must apply the specific statutory tests prescribed under Rule 2(1)(h):

Shareholder Entity Type Test for Identifying the Natural Person SBO
Body Corporate (Indian or Foreign) The individual who holds a majority stake (>50%) in that body corporate, or holds a majority stake in its ultimate holding company.
Partnership Entity / LLP The natural person partner who holds a majority stake, or holds majority stake in the body corporate which is a partner of the partnership.
Trust (Discretionary / Specific) The Trustee in case of discretionary trust, the beneficiary in case of specific trust, or the Settlor of the trust.
Pooled Investment Vehicle (PIV) The general partner, investment manager, or CEO if based in FATF-compliant jurisdictions; otherwise standard individual look-through applies.

3. The Statutory Form Stack & Timeline Flowchart

  1. Notice of Inquiry (Form BEN-4): Every reporting company must proactively review its Register of Members. Where any member is not an individual (holds ≥10%), the company is statutorily obligated under Section 90(5) to serve a notice in Form BEN-4 seeking beneficial ownership disclosure.
  2. Declaration by Individual (Form BEN-1): The individual SBO must furnish a signed declaration in Form BEN-1 to the company within 30 days of acquiring SBO status or any change therein.
  3. E-Filing with Registrar (Form BEN-2): Within 30 days of receiving Form BEN-1, the company must file Form BEN-2 on the MCA V3 portal along with professional certification by a PCS/CA.
  4. Register of SBOs (Form BEN-3): The company must maintain an internal statutory register in Form BEN-3 at its registered office, indexed and open for inspection.

4. RoC Adjudication Orders & Statutory Penalties

The Registrars of Companies (notably RoC Delhi, RoC Mumbai, and RoC Bangalore) have actively cracked down on corporate subsidiaries and startup entities that failed to issue Form BEN-4 notices.

  • Individual SBO Penalty [Section 90(10)]: Failure by an individual to make a declaration attracts an immediate fine of ₹50,000 plus ₹1,000 per day of continued default (up to ₹2,00,000).
  • Company Penalty [Section 90(11)]: Failure by the company to maintain Form BEN-3 or file Form BEN-2 attracts a penalty of ₹1,00,000 to ₹5,00,000 plus ₹1,000 per day for continuing default on the company and every officer in default.
  • NCLT Application [Section 90(7)]: If a shareholder fails to respond to Form BEN-4 within 30 days, the company is mandated to apply to the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) within 15 days to freeze all voting rights and dividend distributions attached to those shares.
