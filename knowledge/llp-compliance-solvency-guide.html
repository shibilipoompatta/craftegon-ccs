LLP Statement of Account, Solvency (Form 8) & Annual Return (Form 11) Guide

A regulatory compliance guide for Limited Liability Partnerships detailing fixed statutory filing dates, designated partner solvency certifications, statutory audit thresholds, and penalty mitigation under the amended LLP Act, 2008.

1. The Dual Pillars of Mandatory LLP Filings

Unlike private limited companies whose annual filing cycles are pegged to the date of their Annual General Meeting (AGM), Limited Liability Partnerships operate on fixed statutory calendar deadlines every financial year regardless of partner meetings:

Statutory Form Statutory Content & Purpose Governing Section & Rule Annual Due Date
Form 11 Annual Return: Summary of management, partner details, contribution amounts, and business operations. Section 35, Rule 25(1) May 30th (Within 60 days of FY close)
Form 8 Statement of Account & Solvency: Balance sheet, P&L account, and designated partners' formal solvency declaration. Section 34(2), Rule 24 October 30th (Within 30 days of 6 months from FY close)

2. Statutory Audit Exemption & Mandatory Audit Thresholds

One of the primary benefits of the LLP structure is statutory audit exemption for micro and early-stage enterprises. Under Rule 24(8) of the LLP Rules, 2009, an LLP is exempt from mandatory accounts audit by a Chartered Accountant UNLESS it crosses either of the following thresholds:

  • Its annual turnover exceeds ₹40 Lakhs in any financial year; OR
  • Its aggregate partner capital contribution exceeds ₹25 Lakhs.
Certification Standard: If the LLP's turnover or capital is below the audit thresholds, Form 8 can be certified directly by the Designated Partners. Once the threshold is breached, Form 8 must be certified by the statutory auditor (Chartered Accountant in practice).

3. Decriminalization & The Compounding Late Fee Framework

Under the LLP (Amendment) Act, 2021, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs decriminalized minor procedural filing defaults for LLPs, transitioning them from criminal court complaints to an In-House Adjudication Mechanism (IAM).

However, statutory daily late fees under Section 69 remain stringent:

  • Daily Compounding Fee: Delayed filing of Form 11 or Form 8 attracts a mandatory late fee of ₹100 per day per form.
  • No Maximum Upper Ceiling for Standard LLPs: For standard LLPs, the late fee compounds indefinitely until the default is regularized, frequently accumulating into hundreds of thousands of rupees on neglected entities.
  • Small LLP Relief: Under the amended framework, a 'Small LLP' (Contribution ≤ ₹25 Lakhs and Turnover ≤ ₹40 Lakhs) benefits from capped additional fees and half the penalty rates applicable to standard entities.

4. Essential Event-Based LLP Filings

In addition to annual returns, designated partners must ensure event-based statutory filings are executed within 30 days of occurrence:

  • Form 3 (Information on LLP Agreement): Mandatory filing within 30 days of incorporation or any subsequent amendment, capital alteration, profit-sharing change, or clause addition.
  • Form 4 (Partner Appointment & Cessation): Mandatory filing within 30 days of appointment, resignation, retirement, or removal of any Partner or Designated Partner.
  • Annual DIR-3 KYC: Every individual holding a Designated Partner Identification Number (DPIN/DIN) must file DIR-3 KYC annually by September 30th to prevent DPIN deactivation.
