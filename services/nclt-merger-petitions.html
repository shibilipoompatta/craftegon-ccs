NCLT Merger Scheme Secretarial & Transactional Execution

Comprehensive corporate secretarial documentation, Registered Valuer coordination, shareholder/creditor meeting administration, and statutory authority reporting for schemes of arrangement, amalgamation, demerger, and corporate restructuring sanctioned by the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT).

Statutory Framework

Sections 230–232, Companies Act, 2013 read with CAA Rules, 2016

Tribunal Forum

National Company Law Tribunal (Jurisdictional Bench)

Transactional Delivery

Practicing Company Secretary (Scheme Desk)

Statutory Notice & Legal Framework: CraftEgon Corporate Services (CCS) provides transactional corporate secretarial, accounting reconciliation, scheme documentation, and regulatory coordination under Indian company law. Contentious tribunal litigation, courtroom bench appearances, and advocacy before NCLT benches are handled exclusively through independent practicing advocates enrolled under the Advocates Act, 1961 on a separate vakalatnama.

Why Corporate Enterprises Execute Tribunal-Sanctioned Schemes

While administrative fast-track mergers under Section 233 are limited to small companies or parent-subsidiary relationships, Section 230 to Section 232 of the Companies Act, 2013 provide the complete statutory legal architecture for large-scale, multi-entity, or non-qualifying corporate reorganizations. A scheme sanctioned by the National Company Law Tribunal carries statutory force in rem, binding all shareholders, creditors, and government authorities once confirmed.

Complex Corporate Amalgamations Mergers between unrelated unlisted public or private companies with substantial asset bases, varied debt instruments, or institutional equity structures exceeding Section 233 eligibility.
Demergers, Spin-Offs & Carve-Outs Hiving off distinct business undertakings or product verticals into separate legal entities to unlock enterprise valuation, streamline operations, or prepare for strategic investment.
Capital & Debt Restructuring Reorganization of share capital, cancellation of paid-up capital not represented by assets under Section 66 combined with compromise arrangements with secured financial creditors.
Cross-Border Restructuring (Section 234) Inbound and outbound mergers involving foreign entities subject to prior approval from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and compliance with FEMA Regulations.

Comparative Architecture: Fast-Track (Section 233) vs NCLT Regular Scheme (Sections 230–232)

Sanctioning Authority Regional Director (RD), Ministry of Corporate Affairs National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT Bench)
Qualifying Entities Holding & 100% WOS, Small Companies, Startups only Any Indian Company (Private, Public, Listed, Section 8, Foreign JV)

Shareholder Approval Threshold 90% of total number of shares at general meeting Majority in number representing 75% in value of members present & voting

Realistic Execution Timeline 4 to 6 months 9 to 15 months (contingent on tribunal docket and regulatory reports)

End-to-End Step-by-Step Secretarial & Procedural Roadmap

  1. Phase 1: Valuation & Board Approval: Appointment of an IBBI Registered Valuer under Section 247 to issue the Share Exchange Valuation Report. Company auditors certify the accounting treatment under Section 133. The Board of Directors of each merging entity convenes, approves the Scheme of Arrangement, sets the "Appointed Date", and authorizes officers and advisors to initiate tribunal filings.
  2. Phase 2: First Motion Company Application (Form NCLT-1): Preparation and joint/separate filing of Company Application before the jurisdictional NCLT bench seeking directions to convene, or dispensation from convening, meetings of equity shareholders, preference shareholders, secured creditors, and unsecured creditors (supported by consent affidavits where applicable).
  3. Phase 3: Dispatch of Notices & Public Advertisements: Pursuant to First Motion tribunal order, issuance of statutory notices in Form CAA-2 at least 30 clear days prior to the meeting date, accompanied by the Scheme, Valuation Report, and Explanatory Statement under Section 230(3). Publication of notice in Form CAA-3 in one English and one vernacular newspaper circulated in the district of the registered office.
  4. Phase 4: Mandatory Regulatory Representations under Section 230(5): Service of notice in Form CAA-3 on Central Government (Regional Director), Registrar of Companies (RoC), Official Liquidator (OL), Income Tax Department, Reserve Bank of India, and Competition Commission of India (CCI), granting a statutory 30-day window for representations.
  5. Phase 5: Conduct of Meetings & Chairperson's Report (Form CAA-4): Convening meetings under the supervision of tribunal-appointed Chairperson and Scrutinizer. Recording voting results (in-person, postal ballot, or electronic voting). Chairperson files the formal report of the meeting in Form CAA-4 before the NCLT within 3 days of conclusion.
  6. Phase 6: Second Motion Company Petition (Form NCLT-1): Filing of the Second Motion Company Petition within 7 days of Chairperson's report praying for final sanction of the Scheme. Re-issuance of public notices and intimation to statutory authorities for final hearing dates.
  7. Phase 7: Coordination with RD & Official Liquidator: Filing point-by-point secretarial counter-affidavits addressing specific observations, accounting queries, or workforce covenant notes raised in the reports of the Regional Director and Official Liquidator.
  8. Phase 8: Final Sanction Order & Effectiveness (Form INC-28): NCLT pronounces the final sanction order in Form CAA-7. Transferee and Transferor companies file certified copies of the order with the Registrar of Companies in e-Form INC-28 within 30 days of receipt, upon which the Scheme becomes legally operative from the Appointed Date.

Valuation Architecture & Mandatory Accounting Standards Compliance

Under Section 247 of the Companies Act, 2013 and Rule 15 of the CAA Rules, every scheme of compromise or arrangement involving share swap ratios or property transfers must be supported by an independent valuation report issued by an IBBI Registered Valuer.

Registered Valuer Report (Section 247) Scientific determination of fair value of shares and assets utilizing Net Asset Value (NAV), Discounted Cash Flow (DCF), and Market Multiples methods to establish the swap ratio.
Auditor's Accounting Certificate (Section 133) Mandatory certificate from the company's statutory auditors certifying that the accounting treatment proposed in the scheme conforms strictly to AS-14 (Accounting for Amalgamations) or Ind AS 103 (Business Combinations).
Tax Neutrality under Income Tax Act Verification of conditions under Section 47(vi) and Section 47(vii) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 ensuring amalgamation does not attract capital gains tax for the transferring company or shareholders.

Prerequisites & Client Input Documentation Checklist

To initiate scheme drafting and First Motion compilation, the corporate secretarial desk requires the following documentation from all transferor and transferee entities:

  • Certified True Copies of Memorandum of Association (MoA) and Articles of Association (AoA) for all merging entities, verifying merger enablement clauses
  • Audited Financial Statements for the immediate past three (3) financial years, including auditor reports and notes to accounts
  • Provisional Financial Statements (Balance Sheet and Profit & Loss Statement) drawn up to a date not older than six (6) months from the date of application
  • Certified list of Secured Creditors and Unsecured Creditors with individual names, addresses, and outstanding debt values as on the cut-off date
  • Chartered Accountant / Statutory Auditor certificate verifying the creditor list and confirming that no corporate insolvency proceedings are pending
  • Certified list of Equity Shareholders and Preference Shareholders with shareholding registers, folio details, and voting percentages
  • Board Resolutions of all merging companies approving the draft Scheme, appointing authorized representatives, and approving the swap ratio
  • Consent Affidavits from shareholders and creditors (for cases where meeting dispensation is prayed for before the NCLT)
  • Details of ongoing material litigations, statutory investigations, tax assessments, or regulatory show-cause notices

Post-Sanction Execution: Stamp Duty, Demat Credit & Tax Consolidation

Obtaining the tribunal sanction order in Form CAA-7 is not the conclusion of the merger process. The scheme requires rigorous execution across company registry, revenue, and banking portals:

  1. MCA e-Form INC-28 Filing: File the certified order with RoC within 30 days of issuance. Without INC-28 approval, the merger is not legally operative in MCA records.
  2. State Stamp Duty Adjudication: Submit the sanction order for stamp duty adjudication before the jurisdictional Collector of Stamps. Following landmark judicial rulings, an NCLT merger order is an instrument of conveyance subject to state stamp duty (typically 1% to 5% of property value or share consideration).
  3. Share Allotment & Return of Allotment (Form PAS-3): Transferee company conducts a board meeting to allot shares to transferor company shareholders per the approved swap ratio, followed by Form PAS-3 filing within 30 days.
  4. Depository Credit & ISIN Corporate Action: Coordinate with NSDL/CDSL and RTA to credit new electronic shares directly into shareholders' demat accounts.
  5. Statutory Identity Migrations: Surrender the PAN, TAN, and GSTIN of transferor entities; transfer pending input tax credits (ITC) via Form GST ITC-02; update banking signatories and utility contracts.

Frequently Asked Questions: NCLT Merger Scheme Execution

What is the typical timeline for an NCLT merger scheme from inception to completion?
A standard corporate scheme under Sections 230–232 typically spans 9 to 15 months. The timeline comprises: 1–2 months for valuation and board approvals; 2–3 months for First Motion listing and orders; 1–2 months for convening meetings and newspaper notices; 3–4 months for Second Motion regulatory representations (RD, RoC, OL, Income Tax); and 1–2 months for final order pronouncement and Form INC-28 filing.
Can the NCLT dispense with holding shareholder or creditor meetings?
Yes. Under Section 230(9), the Tribunal may dispense with calling a meeting of creditors or any class of creditors where creditors having at least ninety percent (90%) value agree and confirm the scheme by way of consent affidavits. For shareholders, while Section 230 does not contain an identical dispensation clause, tribunals routinely grant meeting dispensations where 100% written consent affidavits are submitted by all shareholders in closely-held private companies.
What is the difference between the "Appointed Date" and the "Effective Date"?
The "Appointed Date" is the specific date from which the scheme legally and financially takes effect for accounting, asset valuation, and income tax purposes (usually the beginning of a financial year). The "Effective Date" is the date on which the certified sanction order is formally filed with the Registrar of Companies in Form INC-28. Once the Effective Date is reached, the scheme operates retrospectively from the Appointed Date.
What role does the Official Liquidator (OL) play in an NCLT merger?
Under Section 232(7), the NCLT cannot sanction the dissolution of a transferor company without receiving a report from the Official Liquidator confirming that the affairs of the transferor company have not been conducted in a manner prejudicial to the interests of its members or to public interest. The OL scrutinizes books, auditor reports, and statutory registers before submitting this report.
How does an NCLT scheme ensure tax neutrality under the Income Tax Act?
Under Section 47(vi) and Section 47(vii) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, any transfer of capital assets in an amalgamation is exempt from capital gains tax, provided: (1) All property and liabilities of the amalgamating company become the property and liabilities of the amalgamated company; and (2) Shareholders holding not less than three-fourths (75%) in value of shares in the amalgamating company become shareholders of the amalgamated company.
Why must courtroom advocacy be conducted by independent practicing advocates?
Under the Advocates Act, 1961 and Bar Council of India regulations, corporate services entities are legally prohibited from acting as litigation counsel or conducting oral advocacy before judicial and quasi-judicial tribunals.
