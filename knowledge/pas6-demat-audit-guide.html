Form PAS-6 Reconciliation of Share Capital Audit & Dematerialization Guide

A regulatory manual detailing mandatory half-yearly share capital reconciliation under Rule 9A, the historic Rule 9B private company demat amendment, depository ISIN onboarding (NSDL/CDSL), and statutory penalty frameworks.

1. Applicability Matrix: Rule 9A vs Rule 9B

Historically, the mandate to issue securities strictly in dematerialized form applied only to listed entities (under SEBI regulations) and unlisted public companies (under Rule 9A). However, through the landmark notification of Rule 9B of the Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules, 2014, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs extended mandatory dematerialization to Private Limited Companies.

Company Category Governing Statutory Rule Mandatory Obligation Form PAS-6 Requirement
Unlisted Public Company Rule 9A, PAS Rules 2014 100% securities in demat mode; obtain ISIN Mandatory Half-Yearly
Private Company (Non-Small) Rule 9B, PAS Rules 2014 Dematerialize all promoter/director holdings; facilitate investor demat Mandatory Half-Yearly
Small Private Company Section 2(85), Companies Act 2013 Exempt from mandatory demat so long as small company thresholds are satisfied Exempt
Government Company Proviso to Rule 9A / 9B Wholly owned government entities are exempt Exempt

2. Statutory Filing Calendar & 60-Day Deadlines

Form PAS-6 is filed on a half-yearly basis. It must be digitally certified by an independent Practicing Company Secretary (PCS) or Chartered Accountant and submitted within 60 days of the close of each half-year:

  • Half-Year 1 (April 1 to September 30): Statutory filing deadline is November 29th.
  • Half-Year 2 (October 1 to March 31): Statutory filing deadline is May 30th.
Separate Form per ISIN: If a company has multiple classes of capital (e.g., Equity Shares, Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares - CCPS, or Debentures), a distinct Form PAS-6 must be submitted for each individual ISIN.

3. Step-by-Step Onboarding with NSDL & CDSL

  1. AoA Authorization: Verify that the company's Articles of Association contain explicit enabling provisions authorizing the issuance and conversion of shares in electronic/fungible form.
  2. Appointment of SEBI-Registered RTA: Appoint a Category-I or Category-II Registrar and Share Transfer Agent (RTA) to manage the electronic share ledger.
  3. Tripartite Agreement: Execute the tripartite legal agreement between the company, the chosen depository (NSDL and/or CDSL), and the appointed RTA.
  4. ISIN Allocation: Depository issues a unique 12-character International Securities Identification Number (ISIN) for the specific share class.
  5. Dematerialization of Physical Holdings: Shareholders submit physical share certificates along with a Demat Request Form (DRF) through their Depository Participant (DP). RTA validates and confirms credit of electronic shares.

4. Consequences of Non-Compliance (Section 450)

Failure to dematerialize securities or non-filing of Form PAS-6 carries severe corporate and operational restrictions:

  • Freeze on Corporate Actions: A non-compliant company cannot issue bonus shares, execute rights issues, conduct private placements, or buy back securities until all existing promoter/director holdings are dematerialized.
  • Freeze on Share Transfers: Existing shareholders cannot transfer their physical share certificates until converted to electronic form.
  • Statutory Financial Penalties: Under Section 450 of the Companies Act 2013, default attracts an initial fine of ₹10,000 plus ₹1,000 per day for continuing contravention on the company and every officer in default (subject to maximum ₹2,00,000 for company and ₹50,000 for officers).
