1. Statutory Foundations: Companies Act, 2013 & Incorporation Rules

Corporate entity formation in India is anchored in Section 7 of the Companies Act, 2013 read with the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014. The transition from legacy paper filings and offline PDF uploads (MCA V2) to cloud-native electronic web applications (MCA V3) centralizes all statutory registrations under the jurisdictional purview of the Central Registration Centre (CRC) located at Manesar, Haryana.

Section 7(1) — Charter Execution & Declarations

Mandates electronic subscription to the Memorandum (MoA) and Articles (AoA), statutory non-conviction declarations (Form INC-9), and strict criminal accountability under Section 447 for fraudulent submissions.

Section 7(2) — Certificate of Incorporation (INC-11)

Upon satisfactory regulatory scrutiny, the Registrar issues Form INC-11 bearing the unique 21-digit Corporate Identity Number (CIN), permanent PAN, and TAN.

Section 10A & Rule 23A — Commencement of Business

Reintroduced via the Companies (Amendment) Act 2019. Prohibits companies with share capital from initiating operations or borrowing until Form INC-20A is filed within 180 days.

Section 12 & Rule 25B — Registered Office Integrity

Requires verifiable physical premises within 30 days of incorporation. Gives the Registrar statutory authority to conduct physical on-site inspections where shell status is suspected.

2. The SPICe+ (INC-32) Two-Part Architecture

Under Rule 38 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014, Form SPICe+ (Simplified Proforma for Incorporating Company Electronically Plus) operates as an integrated web application partitioned into two synchronized parts:

Component Operational Workflow Statutory Rules & SLA
SPICe+ Part A
(Name Reservation) 		Promoters apply for corporate name approval. Two name options can be submitted in order of preference, paired with the mandatory 5-digit National Industrial Classification (NIC-2008) code indicating main business activity. Governed by Rule 8 & 8A. Approved name remains reserved for 20 calendar days. Government fee: ₹1,000 (if filed independently). Includes 1 free resubmission window if defects are noted.
SPICe+ Part B
(Integrated Incorporation) 		Comprehensive company data ingestion: capital structure, registered office, particulars of subscribers/directors, allotment of Director Identification Numbers (DIN up to 3 directors), PAN/TAN auto-application, and AGILE-PRO-S linkages. Governed by Rule 38. Processed by the Central Registration Centre (CRC). Government fee: ₹0 (NIL) for authorized capital up to ₹15,00,000. CRC scrutiny SLA: 4 to 7 business days.
💡 Strategic Tip: Two-Stage vs Single-Shot Incorporation

While MCA permits filing Part A and Part B concurrently in a single submission, established corporate secretarial best practice strongly advises filing Part A standalone first. If the name is challenged by CRC examiners under Rule 8A trademark or phonetic objections, only the ₹1,000 name fee is at risk, preventing premature forfeiture of state stamp duty and re-drafting of linked charter documents.

5. Central Registration Centre (CRC Manesar) Scrutiny & Resubmission Defense

All company incorporations across India are examined centrally at the Central Registration Centre (CRC) located at the Indian Institute of Corporate Affairs campus in Manesar, Haryana. CRC scrutiny officers enforce stringent procedural rules:

⚠️ The CRC Resubmission & Rejection Protocol (Rule 38(4))

  • Two Resubmissions Only: CRC allows a maximum of two (2) statutory resubmissions per Service Request Number (SRN).
  • 15-Day Hard Window: Each resubmission must be cured and uploaded within 15 calendar days of the deficiency memo.
  • Automatic NTBR Status: If defects are not comprehensively cleared after the 2nd resubmission or if 15 days elapse, the SRN status is altered to "Rejected / Not To Be Taken on Record (NTBR)" and the filing closes permanently.
  • The Non-Refundable Stamp Duty Trap: When CRC rejects a SPICe+ Part B application, state stamp duty does NOT carry forward to a new application. Promoters must pay fresh state stamp duty upon re-filing and petition the respective State Revenue Collector for an offline refund (a cumbersome process taking 4–12 months).

Top 5 CRC Resubmission Triggers & Mitigation Actions:

1. Utility Bill Vintage > 60 Days: Electricity or gas bills older than 2 months at the moment of officer examination trigger immediate deficiency. Mitigation: Only collect utility bills generated within the last 30 days before filing.
2. Mismatched Subscriber KYC: Spelling variations between PAN, Aadhaar, and bank statements (e.g., expanded middle names). Mitigation: Pre-audit all identity cards against the CBDT Income Tax PAN verification portal.
3. Object Clause & Name Incongruence: Company name mentions "Logistics" but main MoA clause discusses "Software Development". Mitigation: Align Clause III(a) of the e-MoA strictly with the approved name and chosen NIC-2008 code.
4. V3 Profile & emBridge DSC Token Errors: DSC signature failing validation due to unmapped user profiles on MCA V3. Mitigation: Register director and certifying professional DSCs in MCA V3 portal beforehand using the emBridge daemon.
5. Defective Property Owner NOC: NOC signed without attaching owner identity proof or failing to mention explicit permission for corporate registration. Mitigation: Execute our standardized bilingual NOC with owner's self-attested identity proof.

6. State Stamp Duty Schedule & Zero MCA Fee Exemption

A widespread misconception among founders is that company registration has a single, uniform government cost across India. In reality, while the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) central filing fee is ₹0 (NIL) for companies with Authorized Share Capital up to ₹15,00,000 (₹15 Lakhs), state stamp duty on the electronic Memorandum of Association (INC-33), Articles of Association (INC-34), and Form INC-32 is levied independently by respective State Governments under their local Stamp Acts.

7. Post-Incorporation Mandate: Form INC-20A (180-Day Rule)

Obtaining the Certificate of Incorporation (Form INC-11) is only the first milestone of corporate existence. Under Section 10A of the Companies Act, 2013 (inserted via Companies Amendment Act 2019) read with Rule 23A of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014, every company incorporated with a share capital must file Form INC-20A (Declaration of Commencement of Business) within 180 days of incorporation.

Post-Incorporation Compliance Calendar (Day 0 to Day 180)

1
Day 0: Certificate of Incorporation (INC-11) Issued

CIN, PAN, and TAN generated. Activate corporate bank current account selected in AGILE-PRO-S.

2
Day 0–30: First Board Meeting & First Statutory Auditor Appointment (Section 139(6))

The Board of Directors must appoint the company's first statutory auditor within 30 days of registration. Recommended best practice is filing Form ADT-1 with RoC. Convene the 1st Board Meeting (Section 173) to approve the company seal, bank signing mandates, and share certificate formats.

3
Day 0–60: Share Certificates Issuance (Section 56(4)(a))

Under Section 56(4)(a), the company must issue physical or demat share certificates (Form SH-1) to all founding subscribers within 60 days of incorporation. Pay and deface appropriate state stamp duty on share certificates within 30 days of issuance under the relevant State Stamp Act.

4
Day 0–180: Form INC-20A E-Filing (Section 10A & Rule 23A)

Mandatory statutory declaration verified by a practicing CA/CS/CMA that all founding subscribers have deposited their agreed share capital. Attach bank statement showing full capital credit and external/internal photographs of the registered office with name board displaying CIN, company name, and address.

🚨 Strict Statutory Penalties for INC-20A Default (Section 10A(2) & (3))

  • Company Penalty: A fixed statutory financial penalty of ₹50,000 on the defaulting company.
  • Director Personal Liability: A personal penalty of ₹1,000 per day for each day the default continues, up to a maximum of ₹1,00,000 per director.
  • Operational Paralysis: The company cannot legally commence commercial operations, invoice customers, or exercise any borrowing powers.
  • RoC Strike-Off Action: Under Section 10A(3), if the declaration is not filed within 180 days, the Registrar holds statutory power to initiate proceedings to strike off the company's name under Section 248 on grounds of inactive shell status.
  • Late Filing Fee Escalator: Filing after 180 days triggers statutory penalty multiples ranging from 2x up to 12x normal filing fees.

8. Institutional Recommendations for Founding Teams

1. Keep Initial Authorized Capital ≤ ₹15 Lakhs

Maximize the MCA ₹0 government fee exemption. Authorized capital can be scaled seamlessly in the future via Form SH-7 when venture capital or angel rounds materialize.

2. Audit Registered Office Stamp Duty Before Filing

If incorporating in high-stamp states like Karnataka (₹5,000 per ₹10L capital) or Maharashtra (0.2%), factor state duties into initial capital outlay to avoid unexpected treasury debit notices.

3. Remit Capital Strictly from Subscriber Accounts

For INC-20A compliance, ensure each subscriber transfers their exact subscribed amount from their personal savings bank account via NEFT/RTGS/IMPS. Never deposit cash or accept third-party transfers.

4. Synchronize Trademark (TM-A) Filing Early

An approved corporate name does not confer brand monopoly under the Trade Marks Act, 1999. File Form TM-A concurrently under our IPR desk to prevent passing-off litigation.
