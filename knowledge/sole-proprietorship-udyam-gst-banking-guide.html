1. Legal Foundation: Unincorporated Status, Personal PAN & Liability

A Sole Proprietorship is the simplest form of commercial organization in India, owned, managed, and controlled by a single natural person. Crucially, unlike a Private Limited Company, One Person Company (OPC), or LLP, there is no central statute providing for the “incorporation” of a sole proprietorship. It is NOT incorporated under the Companies Act, 2013 and possesses no separate juristic personality distinct from its proprietor.

1. No Separate PAN Required

Under the Income-tax Act, a sole proprietorship is legally indistinguishable from its owner. The proprietor uses their Personal Permanent Account Number (PAN) for all business filings, banking, Udyam, and GST registrations. Applying for a second PAN in a trade name is illegal under Section 139A.

2. Unlimited Personal Liability

Because the business and the individual are one legal persona, business creditors can attach the proprietor's personal bank accounts, residential property, and investments to recover commercial debts. Founders needing liability shielding should explore an OPC or LLP.

3. Trade Name vs Legal Name

A proprietor can operate under an assumed Trade Name (e.g., “Malabar Logistics”). However, trade names receive zero automatic statutory exclusivity via Udyam or Shop Act alone; exclusive brand monopoly requires Trademark Registration (Form TM-A).

2. MSME / Udyam Registration (Revised Classification Effective 1 April 2025)

Pursuant to Notification S.O. 1364(E) dated March 21, 2025 issued by the Ministry of MSME under the MSMED Act, 2006, India significantly expanded the composite investment and turnover ceilings for MSME classification effective 1 April 2025. Both manufacturing and service sole proprietorships follow identical criteria, with export turnover excluded from turnover computation.

Government Fee & Anti-Fraud Advisory (udyamregistration.gov.in)

Udyam Registration is voluntary, paperless, Aadhaar/PAN-linked, and carries ZERO Government Fee (₹0) on the sole official portal udyamregistration.gov.in. Entrepreneurs must beware of unauthorized commercial clone websites charging arbitrary “government registration fees.” Furthermore, holding an Udyam certificate does NOT grant incorporation or excuse a proprietor from mandatory local Shop Act or Trade Licence compliance.

3. GST Registration Thresholds vs Section 24 Compulsory Triggers

Myth Alert: It is legally incorrect to claim that every sole proprietorship must obtain GST registration immediately at inception. Under the CGST Act, 2017 and Kerala SGST Rules, GST liability depends on turnover thresholds unless triggered early by Section 24 compulsory categories.

Section 22 General Turnover Thresholds (Kerala)

  • Exclusive Supply of Goods: Mandatory only when aggregate turnover exceeds ₹40 Lakhs in a financial year (Notification 10/2019-CT).
  • Supply of Services / Mixed Goods & Services: Mandatory when aggregate turnover exceeds ₹20 Lakhs in a financial year.
  • Voluntary Registration (Sec 25(3)): Startups below threshold may voluntarily apply to pass B2B Input Tax Credit (ITC) to corporate clients.

Section 24 Compulsory Triggers (₹0 Exemption)

  • Interstate Supply of Goods: Selling goods outside the home state (e.g., Kerala to Tamil Nadu) triggers mandatory GST from Rupee 1.
  • E-Commerce Marketplace Sellers: Supplying goods through platforms required to collect TCS (Amazon, Flipkart). *(Conditional ₹40L/₹20L exemption introduced for certain intra-state e-commerce goods sellers post-Oct 2023 notifications subject to enrolment).*
  • Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM): Businesses liable to pay tax under Section 9(3).

4. RBI KYC Master Direction: Proprietary Current Account Documentation

Under Paragraph 28 of the RBI Master Direction – Know Your Customer (KYC) Direction, 2016 (as amended), banks are governed by a dual verification protocol for opening current accounts for proprietary concerns: verifying the Proprietor’s personal OVD KYC (PAN, Aadhaar/Passport/Voter ID) plus Proof of Business Activity.

The RBI “Two-Document” Standard & Single-Document Exception

Standard Requirement: Any TWO documents issued in the name of the proprietary firm from the RBI's officially notified list:

  • Registration certificate (e.g., Udyam Registration Certificate, Municipal Licence / Panchayat Trade Licence).
  • Certificate/licence issued by Municipal authorities under Shop and Establishment Act.
  • Sales and income tax returns (or GST Registration Certificate).
  • Certificate/registration document issued by Sales Tax / Service Tax / Professional Tax authorities.
  • Importer Exporter Code (IEC) issued by DGFT, or Certificate of Practice issued by a professional body (ICAI, ICSI, MCI, etc.).
  • Utility bills (electricity, water, landline telephone) in the name of the proprietary concern.
RBI One-Document Exception Clause: Where a bank is satisfied that it is not possible to furnish two documents, the bank has RBI-sanctioned discretion to accept only ONE document as proof of business/activity, strictly provided the bank conducts Contact Point Verification (CPV) and gathers supplementary information confirming physical business existence. Note that individual commercial banks often impose stricter internal credit/branch risk checklists beyond the RBI baseline.

5. Kerala State Licensing: Shops Act 1960 (0-Employee Rule) & LSGD Trade Licence

Operating a sole proprietorship in Kerala engages specific state-level municipal and labour statutes separate from central Udyam/GST registrations. Applicability depends strictly on physical premises, nature of activity, and food/safety regulations.

6. Direct Taxation: Income-tax Act, 2025 vs 1961 Transitional Continuity

A sole proprietorship does not file a separate corporate tax return. Net profits are clubbed with the proprietor’s personal income and taxed under individual income tax slab rates. Effective April 1, 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 came into force (replacing “Previous Year / Assessment Year” with a unified “Tax Year” for FY 2026–27 onwards), while prior financial years remain governed by the 1961 Act.

Presumptive Taxation & Statutory Audit Thresholds

  • Eligible Small Business Presumptive Scheme (Section 58, 2025 Act / formerly Sec 44AD): Turnover up to ₹2 Crore (enhanced to ₹3 Crore if cash receipts ≤ 5%). Deemed net profit is 6% for digital receipts and 8% for cash receipts. Eliminates complex books of account.
  • Eligible Professionals Presumptive Scheme (Section 58, 2025 Act / formerly Sec 44ADA): Gross receipts up to ₹50 Lakhs (enhanced to ₹75 Lakhs if cash receipts ≤ 5%). Deemed profit is 50% of gross receipts.
  • Tax Audit Triggers (Section 63, 2025 Act / formerly Sec 44AB): Mandatory CA Tax Audit if business turnover exceeds ₹1 Crore (or ₹10 Crore where both cash receipts & cash payments are ≤ 5%), or if opting out of presumptive taxation below deemed limits while total income exceeds the basic exemption threshold.
