Trademark Examination Report: How to Respond to Section 9 & 11 Objections

Author: IP Litigation & Dispute Avoidance Desk Published: 16 November 2026 Status: Active Examination Protocol Reading Time: 16 mins
OBJECTION PROTOCOL

The Rule 33(4) 30-Day Response Mandate: Risk of Deemed Abandonment

When the Trade Marks Registry issues a First Examination Report (FER), Rule 33(4) of the Trade Marks Rules, 2017 imposes a strict statutory deadline of thirty (30) days from the date of receipt to file a written response. If the applicant fails to submit a comprehensive legal reply within this 30-day window, the application is treated as abandoned by law under Rule 33(5). While Form TM-M allows a 30-day extension under Rule 115, filing a robust, substantive legal reply immediately is critical to keep the mark alive.

1. Anatomy of the First Examination Report (FER)

An Examination Report generated by the Trade Marks Registry is divided into three distinct operational sections:

  • Formal Objections: Deficiencies in filing formalities, such as non-submission of Power of Attorney (Form 48), improper applicant translation, or missing Rule 25 User Affidavit.
  • Absolute Grounds of Refusal (Section 9): Objections asserting that the mark is generic, descriptive of the goods/services, or devoid of distinctiveness.
  • Relative Grounds of Refusal (Section 11): The Examiner conducts an electronic search and attaches a Search Report citing earlier pending or registered identical or deceptively similar marks.

2. Overcoming Section 9 Absolute Grounds of Refusal

Section 9(1) objections are raised when the Examiner contends that the mark merely describes the product or service. Effective legal defense strategies include:

Strategy A: The "Suggestive vs. Descriptive" Distinction

Argue that the mark is suggestive rather than directly descriptive. A suggestive mark requires a leap of imagination and thought to connect the word with the product, making it inherently distinctive under Indian law.

Strategy B: Acquired Distinctiveness & The Proviso to Section 9(1)

The Proviso to Section 9(1) explicitly states that a trademark shall not be refused registration if, before the date of application, it has acquired a distinctive character as a result of the use made of it. Submit extensive sales turnover certificates, advertising bills, press clippings, and GST returns demonstrating that the public identifies the mark exclusively with the applicant.

Strategy C: Anti-Dissection & Whole Mark Principle (Section 17)

If the Examiner dissected an individual word from a logo or composite label, assert Section 17. The mark must be judged in its entirety as presented to consumers, not disassembled into constituent parts.

3. Overcoming Section 11 Relative Grounds: Deceptive Similarity Defense

Section 11(1) objections cite earlier identical or similar marks on the Register. The legal response must systematically dismantle the allegation of deceptive similarity:

The Supreme Court's 7-Factor Test (Cadila Health Care):

In Cadila Health Care Ltd. v. Cadila Pharmaceuticals Ltd. (2001) 5 SCC 73, the Supreme Court established the definitive legal test for deceptive similarity in India:

  1. Nature of Marks: Whether the marks are word marks, label marks, or composite device marks.
  2. Degree of Resemblance: Phonetic, visual, and conceptual similarity between rival marks.
  3. Nature of Goods/Services: Whether the products perform identical functions or cater to distinct industries.
  4. Similarity in Performance & Character: Differences in composition, usage, and commercial application.
  5. Class of Purchasers: Whether buyers are specialized professionals (e.g. corporate IT directors) or general laypersons exercising average intelligence.
  6. Mode of Purchasing: Channels of trade, retail vs. wholesale, specialized B2B ordering.
  7. Other Surrounding Circumstances: Price disparity, packaging differences, and presence of house brand labels.

Additional Section 11 Legal Grounds:

  • Section 12 (Honest Concurrent Use): If the applicant has co-existed peacefully with the cited mark in trade for multiple years without actual consumer confusion, the Registrar possesses statutory discretion to register both marks.
  • Status of Cited Marks: Verify whether the cited marks are abandoned, opposed, removed, or expired on the IP India database. If the cited mark is dead, submit a screenshot requesting removal of the citation.
  • Prior Adoption (Section 34): If the applicant's commercial use predates the cited mark's application and use date, the applicant's vested common law rights prevail under Section 34.

4. The Show-Cause Hearing: Procedure Before the Hearing Officer

If the written reply is deemed insufficient by the Examiner, the application status changes to "Ready for Show Cause Hearing" under Rule 33(6). Hearings are conducted via video conferencing before an Assistant Registrar / Hearing Officer:

  • Hearing Notice: Registry issues an official hearing notice scheduling a date and time slot.
  • Authorized Representation: The applicant must be represented by a Registered Trademark Agent or Advocate holding a valid Power of Attorney (Form 48).
  • Written Submissions: File concise written submissions summarizing the case law, differences, and turnover exhibits prior to the hearing date.
  • Outcomes: The Hearing Officer may: (1) Accept the mark for advertisement; (2) Accept subject to condition/limitation (e.g. disclaimer of a generic word); or (3) Refuse the mark under Section 18(4). Refusal orders can be appealed to the High Court under Section 91.
