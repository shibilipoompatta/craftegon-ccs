GSTR-9 Annual Return Filing Guide: Table-by-Table Statutory Analysis

Statutory guide to preparing, reconciling, and filing Form GSTR-9 under Section 44 of the CGST Act, 2017 and Rule 80(1). Includes table-by-table disclosure instructions, Table 6A1/8 ITC mechanics, FY 2024-25 exemptions, and DRC-03 voluntary payment protocols.

Author: Indirect Taxation & Corporate Audit Desk • Authority: CBIC / GSTN (Act No. 12 of 2017) • Updated: FY 2024-25 & FY 2025-26 Mandates

1. Statutory Foundations & Section 44 Architecture

The Goods and Services Tax (GST) regime in India enforces an annual compliance review under Section 44 of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 80(1) of the CGST Rules, 2017. Form GSTR-9 serves as the comprehensive annual consolidation of all monthly or quarterly returns (Form GSTR-1 and Form GSTR-3B) furnished during the preceding financial year.

Rather than functioning as a transaction-creation portal, Form GSTR-9 is an authoritative reconciliation instrument. It consolidates outward supplies, tax liabilities discharged, Input Tax Credit (ITC) claimed and reversed, and transactions of the previous financial year reported in subsequent periods (April to November).

Statutory Text: Section 44(1) of the CGST Act, 2017 (As Amended)

"Every registered person, other than an Input Service Distributor, a person paying tax under section 51 or section 52, a casual taxable person and a non-resident taxable person, shall furnish an annual return which may include a self-certified reconciliation statement, reconciling the value of supplies declared in the return furnished for the financial year with the audited annual financial statement, for every financial year electronically, within such time and in such form and manner as may be prescribed: Provided that the Commissioner may, on the recommendations of the Council, by notification, exempt any class of registered persons from filing annual return under this section."

2. Applicability, Thresholds & Exemption Notifications (FY 2024-25 Onwards)

Under the powers conferred by the proviso to Section 44, the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC), on the recommendations of the GST Council, has issued Notification No. 15/2025–Central Tax dated 17 September 2025. This notification exempts registered persons whose aggregate turnover in the financial year 2024-25 and subsequent financial years is up to ₹2 Crore from the mandatory requirement of filing Form GSTR-9.

Aggregate Annual Turnover Band Form GSTR-9 (Annual Return) Form GSTR-9C (Reconciliation Statement) Governing Legal Provision
Up to ₹2 Crores Exempted (Optional) Not Applicable Notification No. 15/2025-CT dt. 17-09-2025
Above ₹2 Crores up to ₹5 Crores Mandatory Filing Not Applicable Section 44(1) read with Rule 80(1)
Above ₹5 Crores Mandatory Filing Mandatory Self-Certified Section 44(2) read with Rule 80(3)

Excluded Taxpayer Categories

The following classes of registered persons are categorically excluded from the mandate to file Form GSTR-9:

  • Input Service Distributors (ISD): Governed separately under Section 20 and Rule 65.
  • TDS Deductors (Section 51): Government entities and public sector undertakings registered solely for tax deduction at source.
  • TCS Collectors (Section 52): E-commerce operators deducting tax at source under Section 52.
  • Casual Taxable Persons (CTP): Temporary registrations under Section 27.
  • Non-Resident Taxable Persons (NRTP): Filing monthly Form GSTR-5 under Section 28.
  • Composition Dealers (Section 10): File annual Form GSTR-4 rather than GSTR-9.

3. Table-by-Table Forensic Dissection of Form GSTR-9

Form GSTR-9 is divided into 6 Parts comprising 19 Tables. Every table demands rigorous reconciliation between the taxpayer’s audited Books of Accounts, filed monthly returns (GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B), and the auto-populated GST portal data.

Part I: Basic Identification Details

  • Table 1: Financial Year for which the annual return is being furnished.
  • Table 2: Goods and Services Tax Identification Number (GSTIN).
  • Table 3A & 3B: Legal Name and Trade Name (auto-populated from common portal registration records).

Part II: Details of Outward & Inward Supplies Made During the FY

Part II captures supplies upon which tax is payable (Table 4) and outward supplies upon which tax is not payable (Table 5).

  • Table 4A to 4L (Taxable Supplies): Captures B2C retail supplies (4A), B2B registered outward supplies (4B), Zero-rated export supplies with payment of tax (4C), supplies to SEZ units/developers with payment of tax (4D), deemed exports (4E), and advances received on which tax is paid but invoices not issued (4F). Crucially, Table 4G captures inward supplies on which tax is payable under Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM u/s 9(3) and 9(4)).
  • Table 4I to 4L (Adjustments): Credit notes issued (-), debit notes issued (+), and supplies declared through amendments.
  • Table 4N: Net taxable outward supplies on which tax liability is computed.
  • Table 5A to 5N (Non-Taxable Outward Supplies): Zero-rated supplies without tax under LUT (5A), supplies to SEZ without tax (5B), exempt supplies (5C), nil-rated supplies (5D), non-GST supplies including petroleum and alcoholic liquor for human consumption (5E), and net credit/debit adjustments.

Part III: Input Tax Credit (ITC) Availed, Reversed & Ineligible (Tables 6, 7 & 8)

Part III represents the primary battlefield of GST departmental scrutiny.

  • Table 6A & 6A1 (Auto-Populated ITC): Table 6A auto-populates the total ITC availed in Form GSTR-3B during the financial year. Following amendments under Notification No. 13/2025-CT, Table 6A1 reflects granular bifurcation of electronic credit ledger entries.
  • Table 6B to 6H (Granular ITC Classification): Taxpayers must bifurcate the 6A total into inward supplies other than RCM (6B: Inputs, Capital Goods, Input Services), inward supplies from unregistered persons under RCM (6C), inward supplies from registered persons under RCM (6D), import of goods from outside India including SEZ (6E), import of services (6F), and ISD credits received (6G).
  • Table 7A to 7H (Statutory ITC Reversals): Mandatory reporting of reversals under Rule 37 (non-payment to supplier within 180 days), Rule 39 (ISD credit note reversals), Rule 42 (common inputs used for exempt/personal purposes), Rule 43 (capital goods used for exempt supplies), and Section 17(5) blocked credits (motor vehicles, catering, works contracts).
  • Table 8 (The Table 8A vs 8B Master ITC Comparison): Table 8A auto-populates eligible B2B ITC from Form GSTR-2B (previously GSTR-2A). Table 8B draws inward ITC availed from Table 6B and 6H. Table 8D computes the variance: Table 8D = 8A - (8B + 8C). A negative variance (8B > 8A) indicates potential excess ITC availment triggering automated DRC-01B notices.

Part IV: Tax Paid as Declared in Returns (Table 9)

Table 9 records the total tax payable and actual tax discharged during the financial year, divided across Integrated Tax (IGST), Central Tax (CGST), State/UT Tax (SGST), and Compensation Cess. It details payments made through cash ledger versus electronic credit ledger utilization.

Part V: Previous Year Transactions Reported in Subsequent Financial Year (Tables 10 to 14)

Part V tracks modifications made during the spillover period (April to November 30 of the following financial year):

  • Table 10: Supplies / tax declared through amendments (+).
  • Table 11: Supplies / tax reduced through amendments (-).
  • Table 12: Reversal of ITC availed during previous financial year.
  • Table 13: ITC availed for the previous financial year in the subsequent year up to November 30.
  • Table 14: Differential tax payable on account of Tables 10 and 11, and actual payment discharged.

Part VI: Other Statutory Information (Tables 15 to 19)

  • Table 15: Particulars of demands and refunds (total refund claimed, sanctioned, rejected, and pending; total taxes demanded and paid).
  • Table 16: Information on supplies received from composition taxpayers, deemed supplies under Section 143, and goods sent on approval basis.
  • Table 17 & 18: HSN-wise summary of outward supplies (mandatory for taxpayers with AATO > ₹5 Cr to report at 6-digit level; optional for inward supplies).
  • Table 19: Late fees payable and paid under Section 47 for delayed submission.

4. FY 2024-25 Key Reporting Changes & GSTN Advisories

Critical FY 2024-25 Reporting Amendments

Pursuant to the 53rd and 54th GST Council recommendations codified via Notification No. 13/2025-Central Tax, several optional reporting tables have been restructured into mandatory disclosures, particularly regarding GSTR-2B alignment and Rule 37A supplier default tracking.

The primary reporting modifications for FY 2024-25 filings include:

  1. GSTR-2B Binding in Table 8A: Table 8A auto-population is now locked strictly to the static annual GSTR-2B rather than dynamic GSTR-2A, eliminating retrospective supplier additions.
  2. Rule 37A Reporting: ITC reversed due to non-filing of GSTR-3B by suppliers by September 30 must be accounted for in Table 7, and subsequent re-availment documented under Table 6B.
  3. 6-Digit HSN Strict Validation: Automated portal validation now rejects GSTR-9 submissions for businesses with turnover > ₹5 Cr if outward HSN summaries in Table 17 contain invalid 4-digit codes.
  4. E-Invoice QR Cross-Check: Portal cross-validates B2B outward supplies declared in Table 4B against IRN records generated under Rule 48(4).

5. Voluntary Tax Discharge via Form DRC-03 & Section 50 Interest

A fundamental principle of GST jurisprudence is that Form GSTR-9 does not permit additional ITC claims or tax payments inside the form. Where annual reconciliation uncovers an undeclared liability (e.g. short-paid output tax or excess ITC availed):

  • The liability must be discharged voluntarily through Form GST DRC-03 on the GST portal.
  • Under Section 50(1) of the CGST Act, interest is mandatory at the rate of 18% per annum from the due date of the original monthly return until the actual date of payment.
  • Voluntary payment before issuance of a formal Show Cause Notice (SCN) under Section 73 or 74 protects the taxpayer against harsh penalty impositions.

6. Common GSTR-9 Filing Pitfalls & Departmental Audit Red Flags

Top Audit Red Flags Identified by GST Analytics & DGGI
  • Table 8D Negative Variance: Claiming higher ITC in GSTR-3B than reflected in supplier GSTR-2B statements without documentary proof of Rule 36(4) compliance.
  • GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B Discrepancies: Turnover declared in GSTR-1 exceeding taxable value reported in GSTR-3B, indicating unpaid output tax.
  • Failure to Reverse 180-Day Rule 37 Credit: Non-reversal of ITC on vendor invoices unpaid beyond 180 days from the invoice date.
  • RCM Mismatch (Table 4G vs Table 9): Declaring liability under reverse charge in Table 4G but failing to discharge corresponding tax in cash under Table 9.
