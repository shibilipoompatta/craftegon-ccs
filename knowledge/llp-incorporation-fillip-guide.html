1. Statutory Framework & Structural Philosophy (LLP Act, 2008 & 2021 Amendment)

The Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008 (Act No. 6 of 2009) established the LLP as an autonomous corporate business vehicle that bridges the operational agility of a partnership firm with the separate legal entity status and limited liability shield of a joint-stock company. Under Section 3 of the Act, an LLP is a body corporate with perpetual succession, separate from its partners, capable of holding property, suing, and being sued in its own name.

The Landmark LLP (Amendment) Act, 2021 (Effective April 1, 2022)

The Limited Liability Partnership (Amendment) Act, 2021 introduced foundational structural changes designed to enhance ease of doing business and decriminalize minor procedural defaults:

  • Introduction of "Small LLP" (Section 2(1)(ta)): Created a privileged classification for LLPs whose contribution does not exceed ₹25 Lakhs (expandable up to ₹5 Crores) and whose turnover for the preceding financial year does not exceed ₹40 Lakhs (expandable up to ₹50 Crores). Small LLPs benefit from reduced filing fees, simplified statutory compliance, and a 50% reduction in statutory penalties under Section 76A.
  • Decriminalization of Technical Offenses: Replaced criminal prosecutions and court fines with an In-House Adjudication Mechanism (IAM) under Section 76A administered by Adjudicating Officers (Registrars of Companies) and Regional Directors.
  • Relaxation of Indian Residency (Section 7(1) Proviso): Reduced the required period of physical stay in India for a resident Designated Partner from 182 days down to not less than 120 days during the financial year. This aligns LLP residency with the normalized standards applied to company directorships.
  • Substituted Section 69 (Abolition of Flat ₹100/day Late Fees): Abolished the former draconian uniform late fee of ₹100 per day without ceiling for delayed Form 3, Form 8, and Form 11 filings, replacing it with graduated fee multiples under Rule 11 and Annexure A of the LLP Rules, 2009.
  • Accounting & Auditing Standards (Section 34A): Empowered the Central Government to prescribe Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) in consultation with the National Financial Reporting Authority (NFRA).

2. Preliminary Requirements & The DPIN 2-Applicant Limit in FiLLiP

Forming an LLP requires satisfying mandatory statutory prerequisites regarding partners, designated partners, digital identities, and capital commitments:

Partnership Composition

Minimum of 2 partners (Section 5). Partners can be individual natural persons or bodies corporate (Indian companies, foreign companies, or other LLPs). There is no upper limit on the maximum number of partners.

Designated Partners & Residency

At least 2 individual partners must act as Designated Partners (Section 7). In case bodies corporate are partners, they must nominate individual natural persons. At least one Designated Partner must reside in India for ≥ 120 days.

Class 3 Digital Signatures

Every proposed Designated Partner must obtain a Class 3 Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) with encryption and signing capabilities, registered and associated on the MCA V3 portal.

⚠️ Critical Practitioner Trap: Maximum 2 DPINs in FiLLiP

MCA V3 Form FiLLiP permits DPIN / DIN allocation for a maximum of 2 individuals who do not already possess a DIN/DPIN.
If your proposed LLP has 3 or more Designated Partners who do not possess a DIN, you cannot apply for all of them within FiLLiP. The 3rd and subsequent individual partners must apply for a Director Identification Number (DIN) beforehand using Form DIR-3 under the Companies Act, 2013, supported by Board/Partner resolution. Attempting to enter more than 2 new DPIN applications in FiLLiP triggers an automated system validation error.

Mandatory MCA Naming Rules & Trademark Clearance

Under Rule 18 of the LLP Rules, 2009, proposed names must strictly satisfy three statutory criteria:

  1. Distinctiveness: Must not be identical, phonetically similar, or closely resembling any existing company, LLP, or registered trademark under the Trade Marks Act, 1999 (especially Class 45 and business-specific classes).
  2. Activity Reflection: The name must reasonably correlate with the proposed main business objects described in the filing.
  3. Statutory Suffix: Must conclude with the mandatory words "Limited Liability Partnership" or its statutory abbreviation "LLP" (Section 5(2)).

4. Form FiLLiP E-Filing & CRC Manesar Approval Protocols

Form FiLLiP (Form for incorporation of Limited Liability Partnership) is the comprehensive electronic web form submitted on the MCA V3 portal for examination by the Central Registration Centre (CRC), Indian Institute of Corporate Affairs (IICA), Manesar.

Mandatory Statutory Attachments to Form FiLLiP:

  • Subscriber Sheet / Statement of Consent: Form 9 signed digitally by each proposed partner confirming voluntary participation and consent to act as designated partner.
  • Registered Office Proof: Recent electricity bill, telephone bill, or gas bill (strictly not older than 2 months) bearing the exact premises address.
  • No Objection Certificate (NOC): Signed declaration from the recorded property owner authorizing the LLP to use the premises as its statutory registered office.
  • Identity & Address Proofs of Partners: Self-attested PAN card (mandatory for Indian nationals) and Bank Statement / Electricity Bill / Passport / Driving License (under 2 months old).
  • Copy of Board Resolution / Authorization: Where a Body Corporate or Foreign Entity is a partner, certifying the appointment of a natural person nominee.
  • Professional Certification (Section 11(1)(c)): Digital declaration certified by an Advocate, Chartered Accountant (ACA/FCA), Cost Accountant (ACMA/FCMA), or Company Secretary in Practice (ACS/FCS) attesting compliance with all statutory provisions of the LLP Act, 2008.

Upon successful scrutiny by the CRC Registrar, an electronic Certificate of Incorporation (Form 16) is issued bearing the unique 7-digit alphanumeric LLPIN (Limited Liability Partnership Identification Number), alongside simultaneous allocation of PAN and TAN.

5. The LLP Agreement & Form 3 (Section 23, Stamp Duty & Tax Traps)

Special Legal Notice: Under Section 23 of the LLP Act, the mutual rights and duties of partners and the LLP are governed strictly by the LLP Agreement. Form 3 is not the agreement itself; Form 3 is the mandatory electronic filing through which information regarding the executed and stamped agreement is registered with the MCA.

🚨 The First Schedule Default Trap & Income Tax Section 40(b) Catastrophe

In the absence of an executed agreement, Section 23(4) dictates that the default rules set out in the First Schedule to the LLP Act, 2008 automatically govern the LLP. Relying on default provisions creates severe operational and financial damage:

  • Total Disallowance of Partner Remuneration: Clause 2 of the First Schedule explicitly states: "No partner shall be entitled to remuneration for acting in the business or management of the limited liability partnership." Under Section 40(b)(v) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, remuneration paid to working partners is only deductible as a business expense if it is expressly authorized by and calculated in accordance with the registered partnership deed. If the LLP agreement is silent or relies on the First Schedule, 100% of partner remuneration is disallowed for tax deductions, resulting in double taxation at the maximum firm rate (30% + surcharge + cess)!
  • Mandatory 50:50 Equal Profit Sharing: Under Clause 1 of the First Schedule, all partners share equally in the capital, profits, and losses, completely ignoring any unequal capital contributions made by the founding partners.
  • Total Ban on Partner Expulsion: Under Section 24(5), a partner cannot be expelled by majority decision unless the partnership agreement expressly confers an express expulsion power. Without a customized agreement clause, a non-performing or fraudulent partner cannot be removed!

Mandatory Form 3 Filing & Substituted Section 69 Penalty Structure

Under Section 23(2) of the LLP Act and Rule 21(1) of the LLP Rules, 2009, information relating to the executed LLP Agreement must be filed in Form 3 with the Registrar of Companies within 30 days of incorporation.

Substituted Section 69 Decriminalization: Prior to the 2021 Amendment, delaying Form 3 triggered a crushing penalty of ₹100 per day without an upper ceiling. Under substituted Section 69 and Annexure A of the LLP Rules, late filing is now subject to graduated fee multiples (1x to 25x of the normal statutory filing fee) based on the duration of delay. Furthermore, under Section 76A, Small LLPs receive an automatic 50% discount on statutory delay fees.

6. Post-Incorporation Statutory Mandates & Annual Governance

Following the receipt of the Certificate of Incorporation and approval of Form 3, an LLP must establish mandatory statutory records and fulfill recurring regulatory obligations:

Day 1–30

Register of Partners (Form 4A)

Under Rule 22A of the LLP Rules, 2009 (introduced by 2023 Amendment), every LLP must maintain a statutory Register of Partners in Form 4A at its registered office within 30 days of registration.

Day 1–30

Bank Current Account Opening

Opening current account using COI, PAN, stamped LLP Agreement, and Designated Partner resolution. Partners must deposit agreed capital contributions.

Annual (May 30)

Form 11: Annual Return

Mandatory annual return filed under Rule 25 within 60 days of the close of the financial year (due on or before May 30).

Annual (October 30)

Form 8: Statement of Account & Solvency

Mandatory solvency declaration filed under Rule 24 within 30 days of the end of 6 months of the financial year (due on or before October 30).

Statutory Audit Exemption & Taxation Provisions

  • Statutory Audit Exemption (Section 34(4)): An LLP is legally exempt from having its accounts audited by a Chartered Accountant if its turnover in any financial year does not exceed ₹40 Lakhs and its capital contribution does not exceed ₹25 Lakhs. Once either threshold is crossed, a full CA audit is statutory.
  • Direct Tax Obligations (ITR-5): An LLP is taxed as a separate partnership firm at a flat base rate of 30% (plus 12% surcharge if total income exceeds ₹1 Crore, and 4% Health & Education Cess).
  • Alternate Minimum Tax (AMT) under Section 115JC: LLPs are subject to Alternate Minimum Tax at 18.5% (plus applicable surcharge and cess) on adjusted total income if regular tax liability falls below AMT thresholds.
  • Tax-Free Profit Share in Partners' Hands: Under Section 10(2A) of the Income Tax Act, the share of profit received by a partner from the total income of the LLP is completely exempt from tax in the individual partner's hands.

7. Common MCA Resubmission Pitfalls & Expert Solutions

Based on audits by practicing Company Secretaries and Chartered Accountants, over 65% of MCA V3 FiLLiP rejections and resubmissions result from avoidable clerical and statutory errors:

1. Address Mismatch Between NOC and Utility Bill

Issue: Even minor typographical differences between the owner name or premises address in the No Objection Certificate (NOC) and the attached utility bill (electricity/gas) trigger an automatic resubmission query from CRC.
Remedy: Ensure the address in the NOC and FiLLiP Part B mirrors the exact character-by-character syntax on the utility bill.

2. Overdue Utility Bills (> 2 Months Old)

Issue: Attaching an electricity or telephone bill older than 2 calendar months violates Rule 24 of LLP Rules.
Remedy: Always source utility bills issued within the preceding 45 days of final submission.

3. Vague or Overbroad Main Objects Clause

Issue: Including unrelated diverse activities (e.g., "software development, real estate trading, and restaurant operations") under one LLP.
Remedy: Draft focused, cohesive object clauses with clear commercial linkage to the proposed LLP name.

4. Inadequate State Stamp Duty on LLP Agreement in Form 3

Issue: Executing the agreement on generic ₹100 stamp paper in states requiring ad valorem duty (e.g. Maharashtra 1%, Karnataka tiered).
Remedy: Verify the state-specific stamp schedule before executing the agreement; attach the original e-stamp certificate and challan to Form 3.
