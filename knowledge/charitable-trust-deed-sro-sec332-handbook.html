1. The Indian Trusts Act, 1882 Section 1 Exclusion & Public vs Private Trusts

A fundamental legal error made by generic websites is claiming that public charitable trusts are registered under the Indian Trusts Act, 1882. In reality, Section 1 of the Indian Trusts Act, 1882 explicitly excludes: “nothing herein contained... applies to public or private religious or charitable endowments.”

Private Trust (1882 Act)

Beneficiaries are specific, ascertained individuals (e.g., family wealth, minor children). Strictly governed by Sections 3–96 of the Indian Trusts Act, 1882. Does NOT qualify for NPO charitable tax exemptions.

Public Charitable Trust

Beneficiaries constitute the uncertain, fluctuating general public or a broad community segment (Deoki Nandan v. Murlidhar, AIR 1957 SC 133). Governed by common law equity, State Public Trusts Acts (where enacted), Charitable & Religious Trusts Act 1920, and Section 92 CPC.

No Fixed Statutory Minimum Trustees

Central trust principles do not mandate a rigid statutory minimum number of trustees. While a trust can legally exist with 1 or 2 trustees, practical banking, quorum governance, and Tax Rule 181 standards typically recommend 2 to 3+ unrelated trustees.

2. Registration Act, 1908: Compulsory vs Optional Registration

In states like Kerala where there is no Charity Commissioner statute, the legal registration of a charitable trust occurs by registering the written Trust Deed at the Sub-Registrar Office (SRO) under the Registration Act, 1908:

  • Section 17 (Compulsory Registration): Strictly mandatory if the Trust Deed transfers, declares, or assigns any right, title, or interest of value ₹100 or upward in immovable property. Under Section 49, an unregistered deed affecting immovable property cannot transfer valid title or be received as evidence of that property transaction.
  • Section 18 (Optional / Evidentiary Registration): If the trust is created with purely a cash or movable corpus without dedicating immovable property, registration under the 1908 Act is technically optional—however, Sub-Registrar registration is practically indispensable to establish a certified public date, secure institutional PAN, open bank accounts, and satisfy Income Tax Section 332/354 Rule 181 applications.
  • Sections 23 & 32–35: Deeds must be presented within 4 months of execution before the jurisdictional SRO (Section 28 where land is situated or registered office sits), with Settlor, Trustees, and two witnesses admitting execution.

3. Kerala SRO Registration (PEARL Portal) & Article 57 Stamp Duty

Kerala does not have a comprehensive statewide Public Trusts Act akin to Maharashtra's Bombay Public Trusts Act, 1950 (religious Devaswoms have dedicated boards, while secular charitable trusts operate under registered Trust Deeds and Section 92 CPC court oversight). Deeds are registered through the Kerala Registration Department's PEARL portal (pearl.registration.kerala.gov.in).

Kerala Stamp Act, 1959 — Article 57 (Trust Instrument) & PEARL Fees

  • Stamp Duty (Article 57 — Declaration of Trust): Attracts ad-valorem stamp duty at the same rate as a Conveyance (2%) calculated on the initial corpus or market/fair value of trust property settled, with no upper rupee ceiling.
  • Mandatory e-Stamping Threshold: If total stamp duty equals or exceeds ₹1,00,000, Kerala requires mandatory digital e-Stamping; below ₹1 Lakh, non-judicial stamp paper or e-stamps may be utilized.
  • Registration Fee (Table of Fees Article I(a)(1)): Standard registration fee via PEARL is typically 2% of the value/corpus set forth in the deed (plus scanning/archiving sheet fees), subject to official Kerala Finance Act schedules verified at the SRO.
  • PEARL 4-Step Workflow: (1) Online User Registration → (2) Online Document Entry & Unique Token Generation → (3) E-Treasury Payment & Appointment Slot Booking → (4) Physical visit to SRO with Settlor, Trustees, 2 Witnesses, Identity OVDs, and biometric capture.

4. Trust Deed Drafting & Essential Non-Profit Clauses

To qualify for tax exemption under Section 332 and donor deductions under Section 354 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, a Trust Deed must be customized carefully—inserting boilerplate clauses without aligning objects or irrevocability fails tax scrutiny:

1. Irrevocability Covenant

Declares the trust strictly irrevocable so the Settlor cannot reclaim settled corpus assets (essential under Section 61–63 direct tax transfer rules).

2. Non-Distribution & Public Benefit

Prohibits distributing surplus to Trustees, Settlor, or relatives; confirms benefits are open to the public regardless of caste, creed, or religion.

3. Dissolution Asset Lock

Mandates that upon winding up or dissolution, remaining net assets transfer solely to another similarly registered Section 332 public charitable trust.

6. Income-tax Act, 2025 (Effective 1 April 2026): Sec 332, Sec 354, Form 105 & Form 107

Registering a Trust Deed at the Sub-Registrar Office does NOT confer tax exemption. Under the operative Income-tax Act, 2025 (effective April 1, 2026) and the Income-tax Rules, 2026, charitable trusts must follow the unified electronic approval workflow replacing legacy Forms 10A/10AB/10AD:

Section 332 Registration

Governs regular & provisional registration of Non-Profit Organisations (NPOs) for tax exemption on charitable income (replacing legacy Sections 12A/12AB of the 1961 Act).

Section 354 Approval

Governs approval of eligible funds/institutions enabling donors to claim tax deductions on contributions (replacing legacy Section 80G).

Form No. 105 (Rule 181)

Unified electronic application form filed on the Income Tax e-filing portal under Rule 181 for seeking registration under Sec 332(3) or donor approval under Sec 354(2).

Form No. 107 (URN Order)

Statutory order passed by PCIT/CIT granting a 16-digit alphanumeric Unique Registration Number (URN), rejecting, or cancelling approval (replacing Form 10AD).

7. NGO Darpan, FCRA 2010/2020, Amendments & Cy-près Doctrine

  • NITI Aayog NGO Darpan: Voluntary portal assigning a Unique ID required prior to applying for Central/State Ministry grants and MHA FCRA submissions.
  • FCRA 2010 / 2020 Foreign Funding Control: Trust registration does NOT authorize foreign donations. Full registration (Form FC-3A) generally requires 3 years active charitable activity and ₹15 Lakhs charitable expenditure; newly formed trusts require project-specific Prior Permission (Form FC-3B). All foreign funds must arrive exclusively via a designated SBI Main Branch, New Delhi FCRA bank account, with a strict ban on sub-granting and a 20% administrative expense cap.
  • Amendments & Trustee Replacement: Executed via a registered Supplementary Trust Deed where expressly permitted by the principal deed. Altering core public objects or dissolving when objects become impossible may require Civil Court sanction under Section 92 CPC and application of the Cy-près doctrine (redirecting funds as nearly as possible to original charitable intent).
