STATUTORY HANDBOOK MSMED ACT 2006 NOTIFICATION S.O. 2119(E)

Complete Statutory Guide to Udyam Registration in India

An exhaustive regulatory analysis of MSME Udyam Registration under the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006, Notification S.O. 2119(E), composite classification criteria, export turnover deductions, and official government portal procedures.

Authority: Ministry of MSME, Govt of India Effective: 1 July 2020 (Updated 2026) Reading Time: 12 mins
📋

Statutory Transparency Notice: Official Government Portal vs. Advisory Retainers

Udyam Registration is an entirely paperless, automated statutory filing administered by the Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises via the official government portal (udyamregistration.gov.in) at zero government fee. CraftEgon Corporate Services (CCS) operates strictly as an independent corporate secretarial, tax compliance, and legal advisory infrastructure provider offering professional classification mapping, NIC activity benchmarking, PAN-Aadhaar reconciliation, and statutory MSME audit retainers.

1. Legislative Evolution: From EM-II and UAM to Udyam

Prior to July 2020, Indian small business registrations operated under fragmented, legacy frameworks. Between 2006 and 2015, businesses filed the paper-intensive Entrepreneurs Memorandum (EM Part-I and Part-II) with state District Industries Centres (DICs). In September 2015, the Ministry introduced the self-certified Udyog Aadhaar Memorandum (UAM). However, UAM suffered from significant data silos: it lacked real-time integration with Income Tax and Goods and Services Tax (GST) databases, leading to widespread misclassification, duplicate certificates, and unverified investment declarations.

To resolve these deficiencies, the Central Government enacted Notification S.O. 2119(E) dated 26 June 2020 in exercise of powers conferred under Section 7(1), Section 7(9), and Section 8(2) of the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006. Effective 1 July 2020, all existing EM-II and UAM registrations were phased out, and the single, permanent Udyam Registration became the sole lawful mechanism to claim statutory recognition under Indian law.

2. The Unified Composite Criteria for MSME Classification

Under the pre-2020 regime, manufacturing enterprises and service enterprises were subject to distinct, unequal investment thresholds, and turnover was entirely omitted from statutory classification. Notification S.O. 2119(E) dismantled this bifurcation by creating a composite classification criteria uniting manufacturing and service sectors under identical thresholds:

Classification Tier Investment in Plant & Machinery / Equipment Annual Turnover Limit Statutory Condition
Micro Enterprise Not exceeding ₹1 Crore Not exceeding ₹5 Crore Both criteria must be concurrently satisfied
Small Enterprise Not exceeding ₹10 Crore Not exceeding ₹50 Crore Both criteria must be concurrently satisfied
Medium Enterprise Not exceeding ₹50 Crore Not exceeding ₹250 Crore Both criteria must be concurrently satisfied
Rule of Reclassification (Upward & Downward Graduations):

If an enterprise exceeds the ceiling limit specified for its present category in either investment or turnover, it will cease to hold that status and be graduated to the higher category. Conversely, to be demoted or reclassified downward into a lower category, an enterprise must fall below the ceilings of both investment and turnover for two consecutive financial years (Paragraph 8 of S.O. 2119(E)).

3. Calculation Mechanics: Investment Valuation & Export Deductions

A critical legal advantage of the Udyam framework lies in the strict statutory rules governing what values count towards investment and turnover:

  • Written Down Value (WDV) Benchmark: For enterprises with prior Income Tax filings, the calculation of investment in plant and machinery or equipment is directly linked to the Written Down Value (WDV) at the end of the previous year as disclosed in the relevant Income Tax Return (ITR) under the Income-tax Act, 1961 / Income-tax Act, 2025.
  • Statutory Exclusions from Investment: Under Explanation 1 to Paragraph 4 of Notification S.O. 2119(E), the purchase cost of land and buildings, furniture, fittings, pollution control equipment, and research and development equipment is excluded from the investment computation. For new enterprises without prior ITR, investment is self-declared on the basis of original invoice purchase value excluding GST.
  • Mandatory Export Turnover Exclusion: Under Section 7(1) Explanation 1 of the MSMED Act read with Paragraph 5 of S.O. 2119(E), exports of goods or services or both are completely excluded when calculating the turnover of any enterprise for classification purposes! This ensures Indian exporters can scale global revenues significantly without losing domestic MSME subsidies, tender protections, or interest subventions.
  • PAN-Based Aggregate Enterprise Rule: Under Paragraph 4(4) and 5(4), all manufacturing or service units associated with the same Permanent Account Number (PAN) are treated collectively as one composite enterprise. Investment and turnover across all units, factories, branches, and GSTINs linked to that PAN are aggregated together.

4. Eligible Entity Structures Across Indian Commercial Law

Udyam Registration is universally open to virtually every recognised legal constitution under Indian law, provided the enterprise engages in lawful economic activities:

Sole Proprietorships Registered using the proprietor's individual Aadhaar, PAN, and business bank account. Highest registration volume in India.
Partnership Firms & LLPs Requires Managing Partner's Aadhaar, partnership deed / LLP agreement, entity PAN, and registered office details.
Private & Public Limited Companies Registered using corporate CIN, corporate PAN, authorized director's Aadhaar (holding DIN), and registered corporate office.
Trusts, Societies & Section 8 Non-Profits Eligible where the non-profit entity conducts vocational, operational, production, or eligible service activities.

5. Special Regulatory Status: Retail & Wholesale Traders

One of the most litigated and misunderstood aspects of MSME law concerns retail and wholesale trading enterprises. Under the original 2006 framework, pure trading was excluded from MSME definitions. On 2 July 2021, the Ministry of MSME issued Office Memorandum No. 5/2(2)/2021-E/P & G/Policy allowing Retail and Wholesale Traders (National Industry Classification codes 45, 46, and 47) to register on Udyam.

The Crucial Boundary: Priority Sector Lending Only

Through subsequent clarification circulars and judicial precedents (including decisions of the Delhi and Bombay High Courts), it is firmly established that Udyam Registration for Retail and Wholesale Traders is strictly limited to Priority Sector Lending (PSL) credit benefits. Traders are NOT eligible for:

  • Chapter V Delayed Payment Protection under Sections 15 to 24 of the MSMED Act, 2006.
  • Filing recovery applications before the Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council (MSEFC) / MSME Samadhaan.
  • Triggering buyer income-tax disallowances under Section 43B(h) (1961 Act) or Section 37(2)(g) (2025 Act).

For full analysis of trader legal boundaries, review our dedicated guide: Udyam Registration for Retail & Wholesale Traders: PSL Benefits vs Chapter V Exclusions.

6. Priority Sector Lending (PSL) & Institutional Credit Facilities

Under the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Master Directions on Priority Sector Lending (PSL), scheduled commercial banks in India are mandated to allocate 40% of their Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) to priority sectors, with an explicit sub-target of 7.5% earmarked specifically for Micro Enterprises.

CGTMSE Collateral-Free Loans

Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises provides institutional guarantee coverage up to ₹5 Crore (and ₹10 Crore under specialized tracks) without secondary collateral or third-party guarantees.

PMMY Mudra Facilities

Tiered micro-finance credit across Shishu (up to ₹50k), Kishore (₹50k–₹5L), Tarun (₹5L–₹10L), and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 Lakhs for established borrowers).

TReDS Bill Discounting

Institutional bill discounting platforms (RXIL, M1xchange, Invoicemart) allowing MSMEs to auction trade receivables accepted by corporate and PSU buyers at competitive auction rates.

*Important Regulatory Disclosure: Udyam Registration establishes statutory eligibility for Priority Sector Lending; however, it does not guarantee automatic loan sanctions or credit approval. Commercial banks evaluate creditworthiness under independent risk underwriting criteria.

7. Delayed Payment Protection & MSEFC Recovery Architecture

Chapter V (Sections 15 to 24) of the MSMED Act, 2006 arms registered Micro and Small enterprises with one of the most formidable payment recovery frameworks in Indian commercial law:

  1. Mandatory Payment Deadlines (Section 15): The buyer must pay the supplier on or before the agreed credit date, which cannot exceed 45 days. Where no written agreement exists, payment is due within 15 days of delivery or deemed acceptance.
  2. Statutory Compound Interest (Section 16): If the buyer fails to pay within the statutory window, interest accrues automatically from the appointed day at three times the RBI Bank Rate, compounded with monthly rests. With the RBI Bank Rate at 6.75%, the statutory rate is 20.25% p.a. compounded monthly.
  3. MSEFC Conciliation & Arbitration (Section 18): Any dispute regarding delayed payments can be referred to the Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council (MSEFC) via the MSME Samadhaan portal. Conciliation is conducted under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, escalating directly to statutory arbitration if unresolved.
  4. Mandatory 75% Pre-Deposit (Section 19): A buyer seeking to appeal or set aside an MSEFC arbitral award in any court must deposit 75% of the awarded amount prior to the appeal being entertained.
  5. Tax Disallowance of Penal Interest (Section 23): Interest paid by buyers under Section 16 is explicitly non-deductible under the Income-tax Act.

8. Income Tax Disallowance: Section 43B(h) to Section 37(2)(g) Transition

Introduced via the Finance Act, 2023, Section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (effective from Assessment Year 2024-25 / 1 April 2024) fundamentally altered corporate treasury management in India.

Under clause (h), any sum payable by a buyer to a registered micro or small enterprise beyond the Section 15 timeline (15 or 45 days) is deductible only in the financial year in which it is actually paid. Crucially, the standard proviso to Section 43B - which allows deductions for statutory payments cleared before filing the Income Tax Return under Section 139(1) - does not apply to clause (h)! If an invoice remains unpaid past the 15/45-day window as of 31 March, the buyer suffers immediate corporate tax disallowance (adding 25%–35% tax burden on unpaid supplier dues).

Transition to the Income-tax Act, 2025 (Effective 1 April 2026):

With the enactment and operationalisation of the Income-tax Act, 2025 from 1 April 2026, the statutory architecture of Section 43B has been re-codified under Section 37(2)(g). The substantive rule remains identical: actual-payment timing rules continue to govern micro and small enterprise payables without return-filing grace periods.

9. Paperless Data Requirements & Common Registration Errors

Under the Udyam portal architecture, no physical documents, balance sheets, or identity scans are uploaded. The portal executes real-time API handshakes with CBDT (Income Tax) and GSTN. The applicant must prepare the following accurate data points:

  • Aadhaar Number: Linked to an active mobile number for OTP verification (Proprietor for proprietorship, Managing Partner for partnership, Authorised Signatory / Director with DIN for Company/LLP).
  • PAN Details: Valid Permanent Account Number of the entity (or proprietor).
  • GSTIN: Mandatory for all entities legally liable to register under the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017.
  • Bank Account: Active bank account number and IFSC code belonging to the business entity.
  • NIC Code Classification: 2-digit (Division), 4-digit (Group), and 5-digit (Sub-Class) National Industrial Classification codes accurately reflecting active business lines.
  • Number of Employees: Male, female, and other workers currently engaged.
Warning on Fraudulent Clone Portals & Unofficial Intermediaries:

Numerous deceptive clone websites mimic the official government portal, charging unauthorized fees ranging from ₹1,000 to ₹3,500 under the pretext of "official government registration charges." The Ministry of MSME has repeatedly clarified that Udyam Registration on udyamregistration.gov.in is free.
