Annual ROC Compliance in India: Private Limited Companies & LLPs

A practical guide to annual filings, governance records, statutory deadlines, conditional MCA forms and year-round compliance management under Indian company and LLP law.

Reviewed: 22 November 2026 · Applies subject to entity facts and current notifications

On this page

  • LLPs
  • Annual calendar
  • Conditional filings
  • Records & governance

1. Private Limited Company annual compliance

Companies incorporated under the Companies Act, 2013 must maintain books and statutory records, prepare financial statements, complete applicable audit and governance steps, hold an AGM (unless a statutory exception applies) and file annual documents with the Registrar of Companies. The exact obligations vary by company type, size, listing status, transactions and exemptions.

Core annual actions

  • Books and financial statements: Maintain books under section 128 and prepare financial statements under section 129, with applicable accounting standards and audit requirements.
  • Statutory audit: Companies are generally subject to statutory audit under section 139, subject to specific legal provisions. The independent auditor—not a compliance coordinator—issues and signs the audit report.
  • Board and AGM: Conduct Board meetings under section 173 and the applicable rules; hold AGM under section 96 within the prescribed period, subject to permitted extensions and exceptions.
  • Board's Report and circulation: Prepare the Board's Report under section 134 and circulate financial statements and accompanying reports as required.
  • Annual filings: File financial statements in the applicable AOC-4 form under section 137 and annual return in MGT-7 or MGT-7A under section 92.
  • Statutory registers and minutes: Maintain registers, minutes and disclosures under the Act and applicable rules.

Small company classification

From 1 December 2025, the prescribed limits are paid-up capital not exceeding ₹10 crore and preceding-year turnover not exceeding ₹100 crore. Section 2(85) requires both prescribed tests to be met: paid-up capital must not exceed ₹10 crore AND preceding-year turnover must not exceed ₹100 crore. Statutory exclusions include holding companies, subsidiary companies, section 8 companies and entities governed by a special Act. Classification affects eligibility for certain simplified compliance provisions, including the MGT-7A route. Confirm the precise facts and current law before selecting the form.

AGM baseline: Generally within six months from the close of the financial year, with the first AGM permitted within nine months from the close of the first financial year. AOC-4 and MGT-7 due dates are calculated from the actual AGM date, not simply from a fixed October/November calendar date.

2. LLP annual compliance

LLPs are governed by the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008 and LLP Rules, 2009. Each LLP should maintain proper books of account, prepare its Statement of Account and Solvency, file its annual return and report relevant partner/designated partner or agreement changes.

  • Form 11: Annual Return under section 35 and Rule 25; ordinarily due within 60 days of financial year-end (30 May).
  • Form 8: Statement of Account and Solvency under section 34 and Rule 24; ordinarily due within 30 days after expiry of six months from financial year-end (30 October).
  • Form 3: LLP Agreement and changes to it, within the applicable prescribed period.
  • Form 4: Appointment, cessation or change in partner/designated partner particulars, within the applicable prescribed period.

LLP audit applicability

Rule 24 provides an audit exemption where turnover does not exceed ₹40 lakh or contribution does not exceed ₹25 lakh, subject to the precise rule wording and applicable circumstances. The Form 8 instruction kit sets out certification requirements. The LLP's actual contribution obligation and turnover must be checked before relying on an exemption. Tax audit under income-tax law is a separate test.

4. Conditional and event-based MCA filings

These forms should not be marketed as universally mandatory annual forms. Determine applicability from the underlying transaction and legal trigger.

  • DPT-3: Depends on deposits and amounts not treated as deposits and the applicable reporting rules.
  • MSME-1: Half-yearly reporting where the company meets the prescribed conditions relating to outstanding payments to micro and small suppliers.
  • PAS-6: Applies to specified unlisted public companies under Rule 9A; it is not a general filing for every private company.
  • CSR-2: Applies to companies covered by the CSR reporting framework.
  • BEN-2: Triggered by significant beneficial ownership declarations and applicable rules.
  • MGT-14: Triggered by specified resolutions and agreements under section 117 and applicable exemptions.
  • SH-7, PAS-3, DIR-12, INC-22: Event-based filings for specified capital, allotment, director or registered-office events.
  • AOC-4 XBRL / CFS: Depends on XBRL applicability and whether consolidated financial statements are required.

5. Governance records and documents

  • Signed financial statements, audit report and notes to accounts.
  • Board and AGM notices, attendance, minutes and resolutions.
  • Board's Report and annual return information.
  • Register of members, directors, charges and other applicable statutory registers.
  • Director disclosures, DIN/DSC status and KYC particulars.
  • Share allotment, transfer and beneficial ownership records.
  • LLP agreement, amendments, partner contributions and designated partner details.
  • MCA SRNs, challans, acknowledgements and filed form copies.

Secure credentials and DSCs should remain under the control of the relevant authorised persons. Use a documented, permission-based process for collecting sensitive company information.

6. What happens when filings are missed?

Consequences depend on the form, the default, the applicable charging provision and any adjudication or compounding route. Possible consequences include additional filing fees, statutory penalties, director-related consequences, notices, adjudication and strike-off exposure. Avoid generic claims that every late filing attracts the same fee or that one default automatically disqualifies directors.

For companies, section 164(2) includes disqualification consequences for specified continuous filing defaults. Section 248 provides strike-off mechanisms in specified circumstances. Additional fees under section 403 and the relevant rules should be distinguished from penalties imposed under the Act. LLP defaults have their own penalty and additional-fee framework.

7. Why a year-round retainer is useful

A retainer is best structured around an annual entity-specific obligation register, filing calendar, named responsibility owners, document checklist, approval workflow, status reporting and evidence archive. It should define included forms, excluded work, event-based charges, government fees and the professional who performs any reserved certification.

Do not treat an annual retainer as a blanket promise to complete every possible tax, audit, labour, FEMA or event-based obligation. Put scope and exclusions in writing.
