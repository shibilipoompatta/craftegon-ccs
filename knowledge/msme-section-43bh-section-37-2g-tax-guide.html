Section 43B(h) to Section 37(2)(g) Transition: MSME 45-Day Payment Rule & Tax Disallowance Handbook

Author: Corporate Tax Research Desk Published: 16 November 2026 Status: Active Law & Transitional Blueprint Reading Time: 14 mins
⚠️

Critical Audit Warning: The Section 43B Proviso Trap

Unlike taxes, employer PF/ESI contributions, or bonus payouts, clause (h) of Section 43B is strictly excluded from the First Proviso to Section 43B. A buyer cannot save an overdue MSE invoice from statutory tax disallowance by paying before the income tax return filing due date under Section 139(1). If an invoice from a Micro or Small enterprise exceeds the 15-day (no agreement) or 45-day (written agreement) statutory limit and remains unpaid as of 31st March, the full invoice amount is added back to taxable profit for that financial year.

1. Legislative Evolution: Finance Act 2023 & Structural Intent

Working capital starvation has historically crippled Indian Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs). While Chapter V of the MSMED Act, 2006 statutorily mandated 15-day and 45-day payment cycles and penal interest, enforcement was routinely undermined by unequal bargaining leverage. Suppliers avoided taking large commercial buyers to arbitration for fear of blacklisting.

To inject self-executing statutory discipline, the Central Government through the Finance Act, 2023 inserted clause (h) into Section 43B of the Income-tax Act, 1961, effective from Assessment Year 2024-25 (Financial Year 2023-24). By linking supplier dues directly to corporate taxable income calculations, the legislature transformed MSE delayed payment compliance from a contentious dispute into an inescapable corporate tax liability.

2. The Two Statutory Payment Timelines under Section 15, MSMED Act

Section 43B(h) does not define its own payment windows; it imports the strict statutory timelines established under Section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006:

Scenario A: No Written Agreement

15 Calendar Days (Statutory Default)

Where the buyer and supplier have no written agreement governing credit terms, payment must be remitted within 15 calendar days from the day of acceptance or the day of deemed acceptance of goods or services.

Scenario B: Written Agreement Exists

Maximum 45 Calendar Days (Hard Statutory Ceiling)

Where parties have an explicit written agreement (contract, master service agreement, purchase order), payment must be made on or before the agreed date. However, Section 15 explicitly provides that the agreed period shall not exceed 45 days. Any contractual clause specifying 60, 90, or 120 days is void ab initio under Section 24 of the MSMED Act and legally collapses to 45 days.

Defining the "Day of Acceptance" vs "Day of Deemed Acceptance":

  • Day of Acceptance: The date of physical delivery of goods or rendering of services.
  • Day of Deemed Acceptance: If the buyer raises a formal written objection regarding quality or defect within 15 days from delivery, the day of acceptance is deemed to be the date when the supplier fully resolves the objection. If the buyer fails to raise an objection within 15 days, acceptance is legally deemed on the delivery date.

3. The Mechanics of Disallowance & Subsequent Year Deduction

Under Indian corporate taxation, taxpayers maintaining mercantile (accrual) accounting normally deduct trade payables when the expense is incurred. Section 43B(h) overrides accrual accounting by enforcing an actual-payment basis for overdue MSE payables:

Invoice Date Agreed Term Due Date (Sec 15) Actual Payment Date Tax Impact on Buyer
10 Jan 2026 30 Days 09 Feb 2026 05 Feb 2026 Deduction Allowed in FY 2025-26 (Timely Payment)
15 Jan 2026 45 Days 01 Mar 2026 25 Mar 2026 Deduction Allowed in FY 2025-26 (Paid within same FY)
10 Feb 2026 45 Days 27 Mar 2026 05 Apr 2026 DISALLOWED in FY 2025-26; Allowed in FY 2026-27
15 Mar 2026 45 Days 29 Apr 2026 20 Apr 2026 Deduction Allowed in FY 2025-26 (Paid within 45 days)

Note on Row 4: If an invoice is issued on 15 March 2026, its 45-day statutory due date falls on 29 April 2026 (in the next financial year). If payment is made on or before 29 April 2026, it is considered compliant with Section 15, and the deduction is validly allowed in FY 2025-26. However, if payment is made on 30 April 2026, it is disallowed in FY 2025-26 and allowed only in FY 2026-27.

4. Continuity under the Income-tax Act, 2025: Section 37(2)(g)

The Parliament of India passed the comprehensive Income-tax Act, 2025 (Act No. 34 of 2025), modernizing the six-decade-old 1961 Act, scheduled to enter full force on 1 April 2026. Tax directors and CFOs must note that the MSME delayed payment disallowance is not a temporary transitional rule:

Statutory Continuity Mapping:

In the Income-tax Act, 2025, the provisions corresponding to Section 43B(h) are codified under Section 37(2)(g) (and associated deductions clauses in Chapter IV). The core statutory architecture remains unbroken:

  • Direct linkage to Section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006 (15-day / 45-day thresholds).
  • Express omission from return-filing extension provisos.
  • Strict actual-payment timing for claim recognition.
  • Compounding monthly interest penalty under Section 16 of MSMED Act remains non-deductible.

5. Applicable Scope: Micro & Small Only vs. Medium & Traders Excluded

The tax disallowance mechanism is precision-targeted. It does not apply universally across all suppliers:

✅ Covered under Section 43B(h) / 37(2)(g):

  • Micro Enterprises: Investment ≤ ₹1 Cr & Turnover ≤ ₹5 Cr.
  • Small Enterprises: Investment ≤ ₹10 Cr & Turnover ≤ ₹50 Cr.
  • Manufacturing Entities holding valid URN on invoice date.
  • Service Providers holding valid URN on invoice date.

❌ Statutorily Excluded from Disallowance:

  • Medium Enterprises: Investment ≤ ₹50 Cr & Turnover ≤ ₹250 Cr (Excluded by statute).
  • Wholesale & Retail Traders (NIC 45, 46, 47): Excluded by Ministry OM dated 2 July 2021 & CBDT/ICAI guidance.
  • Unregistered Suppliers: Suppliers lacking Udyam registration on invoice date.
  • Presumptive Tax Filers (Sec 44AD / 44ADA): Section 43B does not override presumptive tax schemes.

For comprehensive analysis of why wholesale and retail traders are excluded from delayed payment protections, consult our companion handbook: Udyam Registration for Retail & Wholesale Traders: PSL Benefits vs Chapter V Exclusions.

6. Tax Audit Reporting: Form 3CD Clause 22 & Auditor Verification

Under Section 44AB of the Income-tax Act, tax auditors must specifically inspect and report MSME dues. The Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI) technical guidance mandates deep verification under Form 3CD:

  • Clause 22 of Form 3CD: Mandatory reporting of amount of interest inadmissible under Section 23 of the MSMED Act, 2006. Any compound interest accrued or paid to MSEs must be broken down and added back to income.
  • Clause 26 of Form 3CD: Audit reporting of sums referred to in Section 43B. The auditor must list outstanding balances to Micro and Small enterprises that breached the 15/45 day limit and were unpaid by 31st March.
  • Auditor Verification Procedures: Cross-checking supplier master data with Udyam verification API, examining purchase orders for written credit terms, and auditing bank remittance dates against invoice delivery dates.

7. Practical Corporate Playbook: Vendor Declarations & Year-End SOP

To eliminate unexpected tax add-backs, enterprise accounts payable (AP) departments must execute this three-step compliance SOP:

Step 1: Mandatory Annual Supplier Udyam Declaration

Collect written declarations and validated Udyam certificates from all active vendors before each financial year. Suppliers must certify whether they are Micro, Small, Medium, or Traders, and commit to immediate notification of any reclassification.

Step 2: Written Contractual Alignment (Capping at 45 Days)

Replace oral credit understandings with formal purchase orders stipulating 30 or 45-day payment cycles. Without a written agreement, your finance team has only 15 days before interest begins compounding.

Step 3: 15th March Year-End Settlement Sweep

Run an aged payable audit on 15th March. Clear every pending invoice from Micro and Small vendors due on or before 31st March. For invoices received in late March, ensure payment is remitted strictly within their 15/45-day cycles into April.
