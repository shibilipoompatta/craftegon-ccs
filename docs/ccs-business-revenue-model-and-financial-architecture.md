## 1. Executive Summary: The Business Architecture of CCS

CRAFTEGON Corporate Services (CCS) is designed to solve the fundamental failure of the Indian corporate compliance market: **the disconnect between low-cost, unaccountable digital aggregators and opaque, un-digitized traditional practices.**

### The Core Economic Thesis
1. **Front-End Customer Acquisition via High-Intent Statutory Gateways:** High-volume, high-intent corporate formation services (Private Limited, LLP, OPC, Sole Proprietorship/MSME) act as the customer acquisition engine. While many competitors treat formation as a one-off transaction and discount it to unviable levels, CCS treats formation as a **self-funding customer acquisition mechanism** designed to capture client relationships at Day 0.
2. **Back-End Recurring Monetization via Statutory Retainers:** Under the Companies Act 2013 and GST laws, every registered corporate vehicle has mandatory annual compliance requirements (AOC-4, MGT-7, DIR-3 KYC, GST returns, statutory registers). By converting at least 45% of newly incorporated entities into **Annual Secretarial & Tax Retainer Subscriptions**, CCS transforms one-time transaction fees into predictable, high-margin, compounding recurring revenue (ARR).
3. **High-Margin Advisory Expansion:** As client businesses mature, CCS captures high-value, non-litigation corporate transactions: Founder/Shareholder Agreements (SHA), Trademark IP portfolios, Cross-Border FDI/FEMA filings (particularly for Gulf-based NRIs), and corporate restructuring.

1. **Customer Demand:** Volume of market transactions and search velocity.

6. **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):** Blended marketing and sales cost.
7. **Revenue Potential:** Scalability and market ticket size.
8. **Recurring Revenue Potential:** Ability to generate sticky multi-year cash flow.
9. **Cross-Selling Utility:** Synergy with other verticals.

## 6. Financial Architecture & Capital Allocation

To maintain solvency, unit economic discipline, and regulatory compliance, the following capital governance rules must be institutionalized:

### 1. Zero Escrow Risk on Statutory Pass-Throughs
* Government filing fees and Kerala stamp duties collected from clients must be held in a dedicated **Statutory Disbursements Account** and remitted to the MCA/Kerala Treasury within 24 hours of collection. They must never be recognized as operating income.

### 2. CAC Payback Period Standard (< 45 Days)
* Blended Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) for business incorporation must not exceed **₹2,200**. With an average initial gross margin of **₹4,599** on a Private Limited Company, CAC payback occurs on **Day 1**. The recurring retainer revenue in subsequent years represents pure profit contribution.

### 3. Professional Fulfillment Margin Protection
* Professional fees disbursed to external practicing CS/CAs must be capped on pre-negotiated volume matrices (e.g., maximum ₹1,500 per SPICe+ certification; ₹2,500 per annual AOC-4/MGT-7 package certification), locking in minimum gross margins of 65%.
