### Decision 2: Approved Commercial Pricing Structure
- **Description:** Approve the cost-accounting formula and commercial fee schedule for client proposals.
- **Recommended Option:** Adopt the Cost-Plus Dynamic Formula:
  $$\text{Client Fee} = (\text{Base Coordination} + \text{Partner Execution}) \times (1 + \text{Margin}\%) + \text{Disbursements} + \text{GST}$$
  Display indicative base fees on web pages and quote exact totals dynamically based on entity state and nominal capital.
- **Alternative Options:**
  - *Option B:* Publish fixed, all-inclusive prices on website.
  - *Option C:* Withhold all prices and enforce "Request a Quote".
- **Reason for Recommendation:** State stamp duties and MCA capital tiers vary widely across India (e.g. Maharashtra vs Delhi vs Kerala). Fixed all-inclusive pricing causes margin leakage or financial loss on high-stamp-duty states.
- **Financial Implications:** Secures a minimum $55\%$ gross contribution margin on coordination fees.
- **Operational Risk:** Publishing fixed prices without signed partner fee agreements exposes CCS to margin loss if partner quotes exceed estimates.
- **Approval Action:** `[ ] APPROVED: DYNAMIC COST-PLUS` | `[ ] REVISED SCHEDULE`

### Decision 3: Professional Partner Selection & Master Agreements
- **Description:** Formally select and execute bilateral Master Services Agreements (MSAs) with accredited independent professional partners.
- **Recommended Option:** Contract a minimum of:
  - 2 Practicing Company Secretaries (PCS) holding active ICSI Peer Review certification.
  - 2 Practicing Chartered Accountants (FCA) holding active ICAI Certificate of Practice.

- **Reason for Recommendation:** Ensures dual-partner redundancy (Primary and Secondary failover) for all statutory filings requiring external certification (SPICe+ Part B, Form 3, Form TM-A).
- **Financial Implications:** Partner fees structured as per-filing revenue splits; zero upfront fixed payroll overhead.
- **Operational Risk:** Relying on informal or uncontracted professional friends leads to missed statutory deadlines and zero contractual recourse.

### Decision 4: Professional Responsibility Allocation & UDIN Protocol
- **Description:** Approve the legal demarcation between CraftEgon technology coordination and partner statutory certification.
- **Recommended Option:** Enforce that all statutory audit reports, tax certifications, and secretarial filings are signed by partner professionals under their personal DSC and verified UDIN, bearing sole statutory liability under Sections 448 and 449 of the Companies Act 2013.

### Decision 5: Payment Terms, Milestone Billing & Refund Policy
- **Description:** Establish commercial payment rules and treatment of government disbursements.
- **Recommended Option:**
  - 100% advance on estimated statutory disbursements (pure agent pass-through).
  - 50% advance on professional coordination fees upon engagement letter signing.
  - Balance 50% upon delivery of filing receipt / approval.
  - Strict non-refund of consumed government challans.
- **Reason for Recommendation:** Prevents operational working capital depletion from funding client government fees.
- **Financial Implications:** Zero bad debts on government challans; healthy operating cash flow.
- **Operational Risk:** Offering credit terms on statutory disbursements exposes CCS to significant cash loss if clients cancel post-challan generation.

### Decision 6: Customer Data Retention & DPDP Act 2023 Policy
- **Description:** Establish data retention and destruction schedules for client personal data and identity documents.
- **Recommended Option:** Adopt a hybrid retention schedule:
  - Permanent archival for statutory certificates, MOA/AOA, and government approval receipts (Companies Act Section 128).
  - 180-day automated purge for temporary identity proofs (promoter bank statements, electricity bills) post-incorporation.
- **Reason for Recommendation:** Reconciles Companies Act statutory record-keeping with the DPDP Act 2023 principles of purpose limitation and data minimization.
- **Financial Implications:** Low-cost encrypted cloud archival.
- **Operational Risk:** Retaining indefinite personal data without statutory necessity violates Section 8 of the DPDP Act 2023, attracting potential regulatory penalties.
