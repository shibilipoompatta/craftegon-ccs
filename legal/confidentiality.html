Institutional Non-Disclosure & Information Security Standard

Confidentiality Policy & Professional Privilege

Last updated: October 2026 • Governing Standards: DPDP Act 2023 & Indian Evidence Framework

Institutional Counsel Boundary Disclosure:

CraftEgon CCS provides AI-driven corporate secretarial, compliance automation, and documentation infrastructure. All formal legal representation, tax litigation, and appearances before the NCLT, ITAT, or High Courts are executed exclusively through our panel of independent associated advocates, chartered accountants, and senior legal counsel under the Advocates Act, 1961.

1. Universal Professional Non-Disclosure Commitment

At CraftEgon CCS, client trust and corporate data confidentiality represent our core institutional commitment. All proprietary information, cap table compositions, financial statements, valuation models, board minutes, shareholder discussions, and strategic intellectual property disclosures submitted through our portals are governed by strict confidentiality obligations equivalent to standard institutional Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs).

2. Technical & Organizational Safeguards under DPDP Act 2023

In compliance with Section 8(5) of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, CraftEgon CCS maintains reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal and corporate data breaches:

  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Client records and KYC dossiers are accessible strictly by assigned compliance leads on a need-to-know basis;
  • Zero Data Monetization: CraftEgon CCS never sells, rents, commercializes, or shares client confidential data or business master files with third-party advertising brokers;
  • Encrypted Workspaces: All board resolutions, e-form XML payloads, and DSC signing files are encrypted with cryptographic AES-256 keys.

3. Professional Legal Privilege

Where advisory engagements involve contentious matters requiring referral to our independent associated advocates, communications, legal briefs, and evidentiary dossiers prepared for counsel benefit from statutory professional communication privilege under Section 126 of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872 / Section 132 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023.
