Indian Trusts Act, 1882 — Section 1 Exclusion

Section 1 of the Indian Trusts Act, 1882 explicitly excludes public charitable and religious endowments; it governs private trusts only. In Kerala (which lacks a statewide secular Public Trusts Act), public charitable trusts derive validity from equity principles, Section 92 CPC, and deed registration under the Registration Act, 1908.

SRO Registration ≠ Sec 332 Exemption / FCRA

Sub-Registrar deed registration does NOT confer tax exemption or authorize foreign grants. From April 1, 2026, trusts must file Form No. 105 (Rule 181, Income-tax Rules 2026) under Sections 332 & 354 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 to receive a URN Order in Form No. 107, plus separate MHA FCRA approval for foreign donations.

Frequently Asked Legal & Tax Questions

Do all trustees need to visit the Sub-Registrar Office in Kerala during Trust Deed registration?

Under Sections 32–35 of the Registration Act, 1908, execution must be admitted primarily by the Settlor (Executant) and two attesting witnesses. While trustees sign the acceptance clause in the deed, physical presence of the Settlor (carrying original Aadhaar/PAN for biometric verification on PEARL) is critical; local SRO practices may additionally verify attending trustee IDs or registered Power of Attorney if absent.

What is the difference between legacy Form 10A/10AB and the current Form 105?

With the commencement of the Income-tax Act, 2025 on April 1, 2026 and the Income-tax Rules, 2026 (Rule 181), non-profit tax exemption (Section 332) and donor deduction approvals (Section 354) transitioned from legacy Form 10A/10AB (Sections 12AB/80G) to the unified electronic Form No. 105, with approvals issuing a 16-digit URN via Form No. 107.

Can an irrevocable Public Charitable Trust be amended or dissolved later?

Administrative provisions and trustee appointments can be modified via a registered Supplementary Trust Deed if the original deed empowers trustees to amend. However, core charitable objects and irrevocable trust property cannot be diverted to private individuals—if original charitable objects fail or become impossible, Civil Court approval under Section 92 CPC applies the Cy-près doctrine to redirect remaining funds to similar charitable purposes.
