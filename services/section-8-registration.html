Statutory Pillars & The Non-Profit Corporate Shield

A Section 8 Company represents the gold standard of non-profit institutional governance in India. Under Section 8(1) of the Companies Act, 2013, it must satisfy three cumulative, non-negotiable statutory tests:

1. Charitable Objects (Sec 8(1)(a))

Exclusive promotion of commerce, art, science, sports, education, research, social welfare, religion, charity, protection of the environment, or allied public causes.

2. Mandatory Reinvestment (Sec 8(1)(b))

All revenue, surplus, donations, and interest income must be plowed back solely into organizational charitable objectives.

3. Absolute Dividend Ban (Sec 8(1)(c))

Strict, perpetual statutory prohibition against paying any dividend, bonus, or return of capital to members.

Legal Clarification: Earning Revenue is Permissible

A Section 8 Company is not prohibited from generating operational revenue, charging fees for services, or building financial reserves. The law only restricts the application of profits: profits cannot be shared with promoters or members, but must be dedicated entirely to the non-profit mission.

Constitutional Documents: Form INC-13 (MOA) & The Statutory Asset Lock

The Memorandum of Association must be drafted in the prescribed statutory format of Form INC-13 under Rule 19(2). It enshrines four irrevocable public-trust covenants:

  • Mandatory Income Reinvestment (Clause 4): Directs that all property, income, and surpluses are applied solely to stated charitable objects.
  • Non-Distribution of Profit (Clause 5): Completely bars dividend, bonus, or capital returns to members.
  • Director Remuneration Safeguards (Clause 6 & 7): Directors cannot receive salaries or remuneration in their capacity as directors, except out-of-pocket expenses, reasonable rent for premises leased to the company, or reasonable interest on borrowed money.
  • The Statutory Asset Lock (Clause 10 / Section 8(9)): Upon dissolution or winding up, surplus assets remaining after clearing all liabilities can never be distributed to members. Surplus assets must be transferred to another Section 8 company with similar objects, or credited to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Fund.

Post-Incorporation Non-Profit Governance: 12AB, 80G, CSR-1 & FCRA

Incorporation under Section 8 grants corporate identity, but tax exemptions and funding accreditations require four separate statutory registrations:

Income Tax Exemption

Section 12AB Registration

Exempts voluntary contributions and non-profit income from corporate tax. Applied via Form 10A (3-year provisional) and Form 10AB (5-year regular).

Donor Tax Deduction

Section 80G Approval

Enables donors to claim a 50% deduction from taxable income. Requires annual filing of Form 10BD donation returns and issuance of Form 10BE certificates.

Corporate CSR Grants

Form CSR-1 Registration

Mandatory MCA accreditation to receive corporate CSR funding. Third-party entities require a 3-year established track record under Rule 4(1)(c).

Foreign Funding

FCRA 2020 Compliance

Mandatory clearance from MHA to receive international donations. Requires designated SBI New Delhi account, 20% admin cap, and strict ban on sub-granting.

Frequently Asked Questions (Section 8 FAQs)

Is Form INC-12 required to register a new Section 8 Company?

No. For a brand new Section 8 Company, licensing is integrated directly into the SPICe+ (INC-32) Part B webform under Rule 19. Form INC-12 is only used under Rule 20 when an existing commercial company converts into a Section 8 non-profit company.

Does Section 8 registration automatically grant income tax exemption?

No. Registration under Section 8 of the Companies Act provides corporate personality only. Tax exemption on voluntary contributions requires a separate application under Section 12AB (Form 10A/10AB), and donor deductions require approval under Section 80G from the Income Tax Department.

Can a Section 8 Company pay salaries to its directors?

Under Form INC-13 Clause 7, directors cannot receive remuneration in their capacity as directors, except out-of-pocket expenses, reasonable rent on premises leased to the company, or reasonable interest on loans. Reasonable remuneration can only be paid for actual executive/professional services rendered, subject to board approval and Section 197 / Section 8 statutory guidelines.

Can a Section 8 Company receive foreign funding immediately upon incorporation?

No. Foreign contributions are governed by the Ministry of Home Affairs under FCRA 2010. Receiving foreign donations requires either full FCRA registration (which mandates 3 years of operations and ₹15 Lakhs charitable spend) or Prior Permission (Form FC-3B) for a specific donor and project.

What happens to assets when a Section 8 Company is dissolved?

Under Section 8(9) and Clause 10 of Form INC-13, surplus assets cannot be distributed to members under any circumstances. They must be transferred to another Section 8 company having similar objects, or credited to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Fund.
