MCA Imposes Penalties Up to ₹11 Lakhs: Deep Dive into Section 117 & Form MGT-14 Defaults

How Registrars of Companies (RoC) across Mumbai, Delhi, and Bangalore are leveraging automated MCA V3 data cross-checks to penalize private companies and directors personally for failing to file Board Resolutions under Section 179(3).

1. The Statutory Framework: Section 117(1) read with Section 179(3)

Under the Companies Act, 2013, corporate compliance is governed by strict e-filing mandates for critical corporate actions. Specifically, Section 117(1) requires every company to file a copy of every resolution or agreement specified under sub-section (3) with the Registrar of Companies within 30 days of passing, using Form MGT-14.

While public companies are subject to comprehensive filing requirements, private companies are frequently targeted for failure to file resolutions passed under Section 179(3), including:

  • Approving financial statements, Director's Reports, and annual forecasts;
  • Borrowing money exceeding specified thresholds under Section 180(1)(c);
  • Investing funds or granting loans, guarantees, or securities under Section 186;
  • Approving mergers, amalgamations, demergers, or acquisitions;
  • Authorizing private placement offers under Section 42 / Rule 14 of PAS Rules.

Statutory Penalty Metric under Section 117(2)

• Company: Fixed base penalty of ₹10,000 plus a continuing penalty of ₹100 per day during which the default continues, subject to a maximum of ₹2,00,000.
• Every Officer in Default (Directors): Fixed base penalty of ₹10,000 plus a continuing penalty of ₹100 per day, subject to a maximum of ₹50,000 per director per resolution. In companies with multiple defaulting resolutions spanning consecutive financial years, cumulative penalties have reached ₹11,00,000 across company and director holdings.

2. Real-World Adjudication Mechanics: How the RoC Catches Defaulting Companies

Historically, RoC inquiries relied on physical inspections or whistleblower complaints. Under the modern MCA21 Version 3 system, the Ministry has integrated automated analytical crawlers that flag discrepancies between:

  1. Form AOC-4 Financial Notes: Disclosures disclosing new term loans, inter-corporate deposits (ICDs), or equity issuances that lack corresponding MGT-14 Service Request Numbers (SRNs) in the MCA index;
  2. Form PAS-3 Share Allotment: Allotments made without prior filing of the special resolution approving private placement in MGT-14;
  3. Statutory Auditor CARO 2020 Reports: Auditor qualification notes highlighting unfiled board resolutions or non-compliance with Section 185/186.

Upon automated detection, the RoC issues an Electronic Show Cause Notice (SCN) under Section 454(4). Failure to respond within 30 days triggers an ex-parte Adjudication Order imposing personal financial recovery against directors' personal bank accounts.

3. Operational Defense Checklist: Pre-empting Section 454 Adjudication

Corporate boards and compliance managers must implement this 6-step pre-adjudication audit protocol:

1
Audit Past 3 Years' Board Minutes: Reconcile all resolutions passed under Section 179(3) against MCA portal filings to detect missing MGT-14 records before RoC automated scrutiny notices are generated.
2
Apply for Section 446B Small Company Relief: If the entity qualifies as a Small Company under Section 2(85) (paid-up capital ≤ ₹4 Cr and turnover ≤ ₹40 Cr), penalty liabilities are statutorily capped at 50% of regular amounts.
3
File Condonation of Delay under Section 460: For resolutions delayed beyond statutory windows, file Form CG-1 with Regional Director (RD) before RoC issues Section 454 Adjudication Notice.
4
Execute Voluntary Compounding under Section 441: Where compoundable violations have occurred, initiate suo-motu compounding before NCLT or Regional Director to immunize directors from criminal disqualification.
5
Maintain Form MBP-1 & DIR-8 Dossiers: Annually collect and document general notices of director interest under Section 184(1) and non-disqualification declarations under Section 164(2).
6
Automate Secretarial Calendar Tracking: Enforce hard 15-day filing triggers for all post-board meeting statutory compliances to eliminate reliance on manual reminders.
