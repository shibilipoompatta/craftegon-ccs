Angel Tax Abolition in India: Section 56(2)(viib), Legal Changes & Startup Implications

A rigorous tax and valuation guide explaining the legislative repeal of Section 56(2)(viib) effective April 1, 2025, why historical Form 2 declarations are now obsolete for current fundraising, how pre-2025 open scrutiny proceedings remain active, and why Section 68 cash credit checks plus FEMA pricing floors still mandate formal CA/Merchant Banker valuations.

Last Statutory Review: November 2026 • Jurisdiction: Republic of India

1. What Angel Tax Was & Its Historical Purpose

Introduced in 2012 under the Income-tax Act, 1961, Section 56(2)(viib) was originally enacted as an anti-money-laundering measure to curb the circulation of unaccounted cash through inflated share premiums in closely held companies. Whenever an unlisted company issued shares to investors at a price exceeding Fair Market Value (FMV computed under Rule 11UA via NAV or DCF methods), the excess premium was taxed as “Income from Other Sources.”

Historical Exemption Regime (Now Deprecated): Prior to 2025, DPIIT-recognized startups had to file declarations in Form 2 (under DPIIT Notification G.S.R. 127(E) / CBDT rules) certifying paid-up capital caps (≤ ₹25 Cr) and barring prohibited investments to claim a Section 56(2)(viib) exemption. That Form 2 workflow is strictly historical for new investments from April 1, 2025 onward.

2. The 2024 Repeal & Prospective Abolition Effective 1 April 2025

Recognizing that Section 56(2)(viib)—especially after its 2023 extension to non-resident investors—impeded venture fundraising, the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 amended Section 56(2)(viib) to make it inapplicable to any share issuances executed on or after 1 April 2025 (applicable across AY 2025–26 / AY 2026–27 and carried forward with total omission in the Income-tax Act, 2025).

Critical Transitional Warning: Prospective Abolition Only

The repeal is strictly prospective. Equity rounds closed prior to 1 April 2025 (AY 2024–25 and earlier) remain governed by the historical Section 56(2)(viib) law active in those years. Open scrutiny assessments, Section 148 reassessment notices, and pending CIT(A)/ITAT appeals from earlier years are NOT automatically abated—startups must preserve historical Form 2 acknowledgments and Rule 11UA DCF valuation reports for older rounds.

3. Why Valuation & Investor Due Diligence Still Matter: Section 68 & FEMA

Even though share premium ceilings under Angel Tax are abolished for current rounds, two independent legal regimes continue to scrutinize startup fundraising closely:

1. Section 68: Unexplained Cash Credits

Income Tax authorities actively enforce Section 68 (and its 2025 Act counterpart) requiring the recipient company to establish three pillars for every investor: (i) Identity (PAN/incorporation), (ii) Creditworthiness (ITR/bank balance capacity), and (iii) Genuineness of the banking trail. Unverified angel capital risks penal taxation.

2. FEMA NDI Rules Pricing Floor (FDI)

When issuing equity or CCPS to non-resident foreign investors, FEMA (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules enforce a pricing floor: shares CANNOT be issued below Fair Market Value certified by a Chartered Accountant or SEBI-registered Merchant Banker using internationally accepted methodology—mandatory for RBI FIRMS Form FC-GPR approval.
