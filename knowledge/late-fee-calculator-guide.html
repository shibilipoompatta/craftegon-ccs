MCA Additional Fees & Statutory Penalty Guide: Section 403 & Annual Return Rules

An exhaustive computational manual detailing normal filing fees, the slab-based additional fee matrix under Section 403, the strict flat ₹100 per day regime for AOC-4 and MGT-7, small company concessions under Section 446B, and condonation pathways.

Reviewed: November 2026 • Formulated strictly in accordance with Companies Act, 2013 and Fee Rules

1. Statutory Architecture of Section 403 and Fee Rules

The statutory regime governing electronic filing fees and delayed submissions on the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) portal is anchored in Section 403 of the Companies Act, 2013, read in conjunction with the Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules, 2014.

Corporate statutory charges are divided into three distinct legal categories:

  • Normal Statutory Filing Fee: Base fee determined by the company's authorized nominal capital, payable when the e-form is filed within the prescribed statutory due date.
  • Additional Fee (Section 403): An administrative delay charge automatically computed and levied by the MCA V3 portal for submissions beyond statutory timelines, calculated as a multiplier of normal fees or as a daily recurring rate.
  • Adjudicated Penalties (Section 454 / Special Sections): Civil financial penalties imposed by the Registrar of Companies (Adjudicating Officer) through formal adjudication orders for persistent non-compliance, entirely separate from portal filing fees.

2. Normal Base Filing Fee Schedule based on Authorized Capital

Under Rule 12 and the Table of Fees annexed to the Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules, 2014, standard e-forms filed by companies with share capital attract the following base portal filing fees:

Nominal / Authorized Share Capital Slabs Normal Base Statutory Filing Fee
Less than ₹1,00,000 ₹200 per document
₹1,00,000 to less than ₹5,00,000 ₹300 per document
₹5,00,000 to less than ₹25,00,000 ₹400 per document
₹25,00,000 to less than ₹1,00,00,000 ₹500 per document
₹1,00,00,000 and above ₹600 per document

3. Slab-Based Additional Fee Matrix for Event-Based Forms

For event-based regulatory forms—such as Form DIR-12 (Director appointment/cessation), Form INC-22 (Registered office change), Form PAS-3 (Share allotment return), Form MGT-14 (Special resolutions), and Form DPT-3 (Return of deposits)—the MCA applies a graduated slab multiplier based on the number of days delayed past the 30-day statutory trigger:

Period of Delay Past Statutory Due Date Additional Fee Multiplier Example Fee (Company Capital ₹10 Lakhs)
Up to 30 days delay 2 times of normal filing fee ₹400 normal + ₹800 additional = ₹1,200
More than 30 days and up to 60 days 4 times of normal filing fee ₹400 normal + ₹1,600 additional = ₹2,000
More than 60 days and up to 90 days 6 times of normal filing fee ₹400 normal + ₹2,400 additional = ₹2,800
More than 90 days and up to 180 days 10 times of normal filing fee ₹400 normal + ₹4,000 additional = ₹4,400
Beyond 180 days delay 12 times of normal filing fee ₹400 normal + ₹4,800 additional = ₹5,200

4. The Strict Flat ₹100 Per Day Regime for Annual Filings (AOC-4 & MGT-7)

A critical distinction in Indian corporate law is that annual financial statements (Form AOC-4) and annual returns (Form MGT-7 / MGT-7A) do NOT follow the slab multiplier system above.

Section 137(1) Third Proviso & Section 92(4) Second Proviso:

Where financial statements in Form AOC-4 or annual returns in Form MGT-7 are not filed within the prescribed 30 or 60 days of the Annual General Meeting (AGM), the document may be filed with an additional fee of one hundred rupees (₹100) for every day during which the failure continues.

  • No Upper Ceiling Limit: Unlike event-based forms capped at 12x normal fee, the ₹100 per day fee has NO statutory upper cap. A 300-day delay results in an automatic additional fee of ₹30,000 per form.
  • Cumulative Daily Accumulation: Because companies must file both AOC-4 and MGT-7, a defaulting company accumulates a combined statutory additional fee of ₹200 per day!

5. Excluded Compliance Forms & Specific Distinct Regulatory Regimes

Corporate compliance teams must note that several key MCA filings operate under distinct statutory fee rules completely exempt from Section 403 daily fees:

DIR-3 KYC (Director Annual Verification)

Governed strictly by Rule 12A of the Companies (Appointment and Qualification of Directors) Rules, 2014. Filing after September 30th attracts a flat statutory filing fee of ₹5,000. It does not attract daily ₹100 fees or Section 403 slab multipliers.

Form PAS-6 (Demat Capital Audit)

Half-yearly share capital reconciliation under Rule 9A/9B. Because Rule 9A does not specify an additional fee slab, delayed submission does not attract automated portal late fees, but exposes the company to Section 450 general penalties (₹10,000 + ₹1,000/day).

Form CHG-1 & CHG-4 (Charge Registrations)

Governed by Section 77 and Section 87. Beyond 30 days, charges can only be registered within 60 days with additional fees, or up to 300 days with an ad-valorem fee based on the loan value, requiring RoC/RD condonation.

6. Small Company, Startup & OPC Relief under Section 446B

To protect small businesses and early-stage enterprises from excessive punitive liabilities, Parliament enacted Section 446B of the Companies Act, 2013:

Statutory 50% Penalty Concession: Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, if penalty is payable for non-compliance of any of the provisions of this Act by a One Person Company (OPC), Small Company, Start-up Company, or Section 8 Company, or by any of its officer in default, such company and officer shall be liable to a penalty which shall not be more than one-half (50%) of the penalty specified, subject to a maximum cap of ₹2,00,000 for the company and ₹1,00,000 for an officer in default.

Note: Section 446B applies to statutory penalties adjudicated under Section 454; it does not reduce the automated portal additional filing fees levied under Section 403 on the MCA V3 portal.

Frequently Asked Questions: MCA Late Fees & Additional Charges

Can the Registrar of Companies (RoC) waive the ₹100 per day additional fee? No. The additional fee under Section 403 is system-generated and automatically computed by the MCA V3 portal software at the time of SRN payment. Neither the RoC nor the Regional Director has administrative discretionary powers to manually waive or reduce these statutory portal charges without a formal Central Government Condonation Scheme.
What is the procedure for Condonation of Delay under Section 460? Where an application or document could not be filed within statutory timelines due to legitimate hardship or portal technical glitches, the company can apply to the Central Government (Ministry of Corporate Affairs) in Form CG-1 under Section 460(b) praying for condonation of delay.
Does payment of additional fees protect directors from Section 164(2) disqualification? Only if the backlog returns are filed BEFORE the three continuous financial years expire. If the company fails to file AOC-4 or MGT-7 for 3 consecutive years, the disqualification of directors under Section 164(2) triggers automatically by operation of law.
How are additional fees calculated for Limited Liability Partnerships (LLPs)? Under the LLP Act, 2008 (as amended by LLP Amendment Act, 2021), delayed filing of LLP Form 11 (Annual Return) and Form 8 (Statement of Accounts & Solvency) no longer attracts the historic punitive ₹100/day penalty. Instead, LLPs now pay slab-based additional fees ranging from 2x to 15x of normal fees (typically ₹100 to ₹1,500 total).
