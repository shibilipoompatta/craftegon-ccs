1. Statutory Framework: Section 8(1)(a), (b), (c) Cumulative Tests

Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013 authorizes the Central Government (administered via the Registrar of Companies / Central Registration Centre) to license an association formed as a limited company without the addition to its name of the word “Limited” or “Private Limited”.

The grant and continuation of this statutory licence is conditioned upon satisfying three cumulative, non-negotiable statutory pillars:

Section 8(1)(a)

Eligible Charitable Objects

Must be formed solely for promoting commerce, art, science, sports, education, research, social welfare, religion, charity, protection of environment, or any other similar public interest object.

Section 8(1)(b)

Mandatory Income Application

Intends to apply all its profits (if any) or any other income generated from operations, grants, or donations solely in promoting its charitable objects.

Section 8(1)(c)

Absolute Dividend Prohibition

Strictly prohibits the payment of any dividend, bonus, or return of capital to its members directly or indirectly. The non-distribution constraint is perpetual.

Critical Distinction: Revenue Generation vs. Profit Distribution

A common legal myth is that a Section 8 Company cannot earn revenue, charge fees, or generate operational surpluses. Indian law does not prohibit a Section 8 company from charging fees for training, research publications, hospital care, or consulting services. The statutory restriction is an application restriction: all generated surplus must be reinvested into organizational objects; no surplus can ever be distributed to members as a dividend or return on equity.

2. Critical Licensing Architecture: Rule 19 (SPICe+) vs Rule 20 (INC-12) & The INC-12 Fallacy

A critical procedural pitfall in Indian non-profit practice is the widespread belief that an applicant must file Form INC-12 to incorporate a new Section 8 company. **This is obsolete.**

Statutory Parameter Rule 19 (New Incorporation) Rule 20 (Existing Company Conversion)
Applicable Entity Brand new Section 8 Company incorporating from scratch Already incorporated commercial Private/Public Company
Statutory Filing Form Integrated SPICe+ (INC-32) Part B Standalone Form INC-12
Licensing Approval Route Central Registration Centre (CRC) single-window Regional Director (RD) / RoC approval
Newspaper Notice Requirement NOT required (Direct CRC scrutiny) Mandatory (Form INC-26 in vernacular & English)
Statutory Licence Output Licence in Form INC-16 issued with CoI Licence in Form INC-17 followed by Form INC-27

Why the “INC-12 Fallacy” Must Be Avoided

Vide the Companies (Incorporation) Sixth Amendment Rules, 2019 and the MCA V3 architecture, Section 8 licensing for a new company was fully consolidated into SPICe+ Part B. Attempting to file Form INC-12 for a new company is impossible on MCA V3 because the form requires a pre-existing Corporate Identification Number (CIN). Claiming INC-12 is mandatory for new entities misleads founders and reflects outdated knowledge.

3. Constitutional Documents: Form INC-13 (MOA), The Statutory Asset Lock & AOA

The Memorandum of Association of a Section 8 Company must strictly follow the statutory template prescribed in Form INC-13 under Rule 19(2).

The Four Irrevocable Constitutional Covenants:

  • Clause 4 (Application of Income): Mandates that all operational surpluses, donations, and interest income must be plowed back solely into approved charitable objects.
  • Clause 5 (Non-Distribution of Profit): Expressly bars any dividend, bonus, or capital distribution to past or present members.
  • Clause 6 & 7 (Director Remuneration Restriction): Directors cannot receive salaries or managerial remuneration in their capacity as directors, except out-of-pocket expenses, reasonable rent for property leased to the company, or reasonable interest on borrowed funds. Remuneration for executive service requires express compliance with Companies Act safeguards and tax guidelines.
  • Clause 10 (The Statutory Asset Lock under Section 8(9)): Upon dissolution or winding up, remaining assets after satisfying all debts shall not be distributed to members. Surplus assets must be transferred to another Section 8 company having similar objects, or credited to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Fund. Founders and heirs can never recover surplus assets.

Articles of Association (AOA) Governance Parameters:

Section 8 companies can be formed as companies limited by shares or companies limited by guarantee. Most institutional non-profits are formed as companies limited by guarantee without share capital. AOA drafting must clearly define membership admission protocols, cessation rules, governing board elections, and conflict-of-interest recusal mechanisms under Section 184 and Section 188.

4. Step-by-Step SPICe+ MCA V3 Electronic Incorporation Workflow

Incorporating a new Section 8 company follows an integrated 5-stage procedure on the MCA V3 portal:

Stage 1: SPICe+ Part A (Name Reservation)

Name pre-scrutiny and reservation. The name must incorporate statutory non-profit keywords such as Foundation, Forum, Association, Council, Federation, Confederation, Society, or Institute. Approved names are valid for 20 days.

Stage 2: SPICe+ Part B & Section 8 Licence Module

Comprehensive capture of registered office, minimum 2 directors/members (private) or 3 directors/7 members (public), capital/guarantee details, and DIN allotment for up to 3 directors.

Stage 3: Mandatory Statutory Section 8 Attachments

1. Draft Memorandum in Form INC-13 and Articles of Association.
2. Form INC-14: Statutory declaration by practicing CA, CS, Cost Accountant, or Advocate.
3. Form INC-15: Statutory declarations and affidavits by all subscribing promoters.
4. Three-Year Financial Projections: Estimate of future annual income and expenditure, specifying funding sources.
5. Brief statement of assets and liabilities of the promoters/association.

Stage 4: Linked Forms (INC-35 AGILE-PRO-S & INC-9)

Simultaneous electronic registration for GSTIN (if applicable), EPFO, ESIC, Professional Tax, and current bank account opening resolution.

Stage 5: CRC Manesar Scrutiny & CoI Issuance

Affixing Class 3 DSCs and digital submission. CRC examines the charitable objects. Upon approval, CRC issues the Section 8 Licence in Form INC-16 alongside the Certificate of Incorporation (CIN), PAN, and TAN.

5. Income Tax Dual Regime: Sections 12AB & 80G and Section 2(15) 20% Commercial Cap

CRITICAL STATUTORY DISTINCTION: Incorporation ≠ Tax Exemption

A Section 8 Company is assessed as a standard corporate assessee under the Income Tax Act, 1961. It possesses zero tax exemptions until it separately applies for and obtains statutory registration under Section 12AB (for income exemption) and Section 80G (for donor tax deduction).

Provisional vs. Regular Tax Registration (Form 10A vs Form 10AB)

  • Provisional Registration (Form 10A): Newly incorporated Section 8 companies must apply electronically in Form 10A at least 1 month before the start of the relevant assessment year. Granted automatically by CPC for 3 Assessment Years.
  • Regular Registration (Form 10AB): Must be filed at least 6 months prior to expiry of provisional registration, or within 6 months of commencing charitable activities (whichever is earlier). Processed by CIT(Exemptions) with detailed examination of books of accounts; valid for 5 Years.
  • Section 80G Donor Benefit & Form 10BD Mandate: Registered institutions must file an annual statement of donations in Form 10BD by May 31 and issue donation certificates in Form 10BE to donors.

The Section 2(15) 20% Commercial Receipts Trap

Under the proviso to Section 2(15), if an entity operates under the general public utility (GPU) category and charges fees or receives payments for commercial activities:

  • Commercial receipts must not exceed 20% of total annual receipts.
  • If commercial receipts exceed 20% in any financial year, the entity **forfeits its tax exemption for that year**, and net commercial receipts are taxed at the maximum marginal rate (MMR) under Section 13(10), as confirmed by the Supreme Court in *ACIT v. Ahmedabad Urban Development Authority (AUDA) (2022)*.

6. CSR Funding & Implementing Agency Status: Section 135 & Form CSR-1

Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) under Section 135 of the Companies Act requires companies with net worth ≥ ₹500 Cr, turnover ≥ ₹1,000 Cr, or net profit ≥ ₹5 Cr to deploy at least 2% of average net profits into Schedule VII social projects.

Section 8 companies are the premier legal vehicle for corporate CSR implementation under the Companies (CSR Policy) Rules, 2014:

Prerequisites to Act as an Eligible CSR Implementing Agency:

  1. Mandatory Form CSR-1 Registration: Every entity must register with the MCA by filing electronic Form CSR-1 certified by a practicing CA, CS, or CMA. The system issues a unique CSR Registration Number.
  2. The 3-Year Established Track Record Rule: An independent Section 8 company must have an established track record of at least 3 years in undertaking similar charitable activities (Rule 4(1)(c)). Exemption: Section 8 companies established directly by the corporate donor or by the Central/State Government are exempt from the 3-year track record.
  3. Dual 12AB and 80G Registration: Corporate donors require valid 12AB and 80G certificates before releasing CSR funds.

7. Foreign Contributions: FCRA 2010 / 2020 Regulatory Architecture

Accepting international grants, foreign donations, or gifts from overseas entities is governed strictly by the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) under the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010 and the FCRA Amendment Act, 2020.

FCRA 2020 Core Statutory Standards:

  • Full Registration (Form FC-3A): Requires at least 3 years of active existence and a minimum spend of ₹15 Lakhs on core charitable objects (excluding administrative expenses) over the preceding 3 years.
  • Prior Permission (Form FC-3B): For organizations under 3 years old: permitted only for a specific foreign donor, specific project, and specific sum, backed by a formal donor commitment letter.
  • The SBI New Delhi Mandate (Section 17): All primary foreign contributions must be received exclusively in the designated FCRA Account at the State Bank of India (SBI), Main Branch, 11 Sansad Marg, New Delhi.
  • Absolute Ban on Sub-Granting (Section 7): Foreign funds cannot be transferred or sub-granted to any other NGO, society, or Section 8 company, even if that recipient holds a valid FCRA license.
  • Administrative Expense Ceiling (Section 8): Administrative expenses are strictly capped at 20% of total foreign contributions received in that fiscal year.
