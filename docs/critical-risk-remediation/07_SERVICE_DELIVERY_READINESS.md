## 1. Commercial Reality & The Capacity Paradox

The CraftEgon CCS platform currently showcases **57 canonical services across 15 practice verticals**, ranging from straightforward business incorporation to high-stakes NCLT insolvency litigation, cross-border FEMA transfer pricing, and statutory secretarial audits.

### The Capacity Paradox:
- **Public Claim:** The website presents an impression of a full-service, multi-disciplinary corporate powerhouse capable of handling complex multinational transactions, judicial litigation, and statutory audits across India.
- **Operational Reality:** CraftEgon is an emerging corporate services and technology firm operating with lean core staffing. It does not employ full-time senior litigators, dedicated tax audit partners, or registered insolvency professionals on permanent payroll.
- **The Hazard:** If a client engages CraftEgon for a complex, time-sensitive compliance service (e.g., Form 3CEB Transfer Pricing or NCLT Oppression Defense) and CraftEgon lacks an active, contracted specialist ready to execute the filing, the client will face missed statutory deadlines and CraftEgon will face professional negligence claims.

## 3. Empanelment & Professional Agreement Gaps

### Critical Operational Deficiencies Identified:
1. **Lack of Executed SLAs:** CraftEgon currently possesses no signed Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) or Service Level Agreements (SLAs) with licensed practicing Chartered Accountants (for statutory audits), Company Secretaries (for secretarial audits), or Advocates (for court appearances).
2. **Indemnity & Insurance:** There is no professional indemnity insurance policy in place to cover potential statutory penalties or missed filing liabilities.
3. **Fee Sharing Governance:** Under the **Chartered Accountants Act (First Schedule)** and **Advocates Act**, licensed professionals are strictly prohibited from sharing professional fees or commissions with non-licensed corporate entities. Structuring must follow legitimate facilitation and technology service fees, not fee-splitting.
