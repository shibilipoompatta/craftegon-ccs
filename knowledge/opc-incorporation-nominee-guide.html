1. Statutory Definition & Corporate Nature (Section 2(62) & 3(1)(c))

The Companies Act, 2013 introduced the One Person Company concept into Indian jurisprudence to encourage individual entrepreneurship and economic formalization.

Under Section 2(62) of the Act:

“One Person Company means a company which has only one person as a member.”

Essential Legal Character: By virtue of Section 3(1)(c), an OPC is statutorily classified as a Private Company. Consequently, all provisions of the Companies Act applicable to a private limited company automatically apply to an OPC unless expressly exempted by statutory carve-outs (such as Section 122) or official Central Government notifications.

Single Shareholder vs. Directorship

An OPC can only have one shareholder (member). However, under Section 149(1), it may appoint up to 15 directors. The sole member may act as the sole director, or appoint professional third-party directors.

Separate Juristic Personality

Under Section 9, incorporation creates a distinct legal entity capable of owning assets, entering into contracts, and incurring debts completely detached from the personal estate of the founder.

2. Eligibility Criteria & The 2021 NRI Liberalization (Rule 3)

Rule 3 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014 defines the mandatory qualifications and statutory restrictions for forming an OPC.

The Landmark 2021 Amendment (Effective April 1, 2021)

Vide the Companies (Incorporation) Second Amendment Rules, 2021 (w.e.f. 01/04/2021), the Ministry of Corporate Affairs overhauled member eligibility:

  • Inclusion of Non-Resident Indians (NRIs): The words "and resident in India" were substituted with "whether resident in India or otherwise". As a result, an Indian citizen residing abroad (NRI) can incorporate an OPC and act as a nominee.
  • Relaxation of Residency Period: For Indian citizens who are residents, the qualifying stay threshold was relaxed from 182 days in the preceding calendar year down to not less than 120 days during the immediately preceding financial year.
  • Strict Foreign National Bar: Foreign citizens and foreign nationals remain completely prohibited from incorporating an OPC or acting as a nominee, regardless of their residence in India.
  • Natural Persons Only: Bodies corporate, companies, LLPs, trusts, and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) cannot be members or nominees.
  • Minor Prohibition (Rule 3(4)): A minor cannot become a member or nominee of an OPC, nor can a minor hold shares with beneficial interest.
  • One Person, One OPC Limit (Rule 3(2)): A natural person shall not be a member of more than one OPC at any point in time, and shall not be a nominee in more than one OPC.
  • 180-Day Rule for Dual Membership (Rule 3(3)): If a natural person who is already a member of an OPC becomes a member in another OPC by virtue of being a nominee upon the death/incapacity of the original member, that person must resolve the dual membership within 180 days by resigning or converting one entity.

Statutorily Prohibited Activities for OPCs

1. Charitable Objects (Rule 3(5)): An OPC cannot be incorporated as or converted into a Section 8 (non-profit/charitable) company.
2. Non-Banking Financial Investment Activities (Rule 3(6)): An OPC cannot carry out non-banking financial investment activities, including investment in securities of any bodies corporate.

3. Nominee Appointment & Consent Evolution: INC-3 in SPICe+ Part B & Form INC-4

Because an OPC has only one shareholder, perpetual succession requires a statutory successor. Under the proviso to Section 3(1) and Rule 4 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014, the subscriber must nominate a natural person who will become the sole member if the founder dies or becomes incapacitated.

Mandatory Procedural Alert: The 2023 MCA V3 Transition of Form INC-3

Under the Companies (Incorporation) Amendment Rules, 2023 (effective 23 January 2023) and the migration to the MCA21 V3 ecosystem:

  • No Standalone INC-3 E-Form at Incorporation: In the legacy V2 portal, a separate e-Form INC-3 was executed. In the current MCA V3 architecture, nominee consent and declarations are captured directly within the integrated web-form SPICe+ (INC-32) Part B.
  • Portal Submission: The subscriber inputs the nominee’s PAN/DIN, father’s name, residential address, email, and mobile number, and attaches the nominee’s self-attested PAN, identity proof, address proof, and written consent declaration to SPICe+ Part B.
  • Subsequent Replacement / Withdrawal (Form INC-4): Form INC-3 is superseded at incorporation. However, whenever the nominee is changed voluntarily by the member or withdraws consent post-incorporation, the company must file Form INC-4 with the ROC within 30 days.

Nominee Succession Mechanics (Death or Incapacity)

  1. Upon the death or incapacity of the sole member, the nominee immediately becomes entitled to all shares and corporate rights.
  2. The company must file Form INC-4 within 30 days intimating the transmission of shares and the cessation of the deceased member.
  3. Within 15 days of becoming the new member, the former nominee must nominate another natural Indian citizen as the new nominee and file an additional Form INC-4.

4. SPICe+ MCA V3 Electronic Incorporation Workflow

OPC incorporation is executed through the unified SPICe+ (INC-32) suite on the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA V3) portal:

Step 1: SPICe+ Part A (Name Reservation)

Reservation of proposed company name with Central Registration Centre (CRC). The name must end with the statutory suffix “(OPC) Private Limited”. Filing fee is ₹1,000 for standalone Part A, or integrated free in Part B.

Step 2: SPICe+ Part B (Integrated Incorporation)

Detailed capture of capital structure, sole member details, nominee particulars and consent, registered office address, and DIN allotment for up to 3 proposed directors.

Step 3: Linked Statutory E-Forms

e-MOA (Form INC-33): Electronic Memorandum of Association with Object Clauses and Nominee particulars.
e-AOA (Form INC-34): Electronic Articles of Association regulating internal governance.
AGILE-PRO-S (Form INC-35): Simultaneous registration for GSTIN, EPFO, ESIC, Professional Tax (state-specific), Bank Account opening, and Shop & Establishment.

Step 4: Professional Certification (Section 7(1)(b))

Mandatory digital certification by an Advocate, Chartered Accountant (FCA/ACA), Cost Accountant (FCMA/ACMA), or Company Secretary in Practice (FCS/ACS) verifying that all requirements of the Act have been complied with.

5. Statutory Compliance Carve-Outs & Operational Exemptions

An OPC enjoys significant statutory exemptions compared to multi-member private limited companies:

Statutory Provision Rule Applicable to OPC Practical Secretarial Relief
Annual General Meeting (Section 96) Completely Exempt No requirement to hold an AGM, circulate notice, or establish quorum.
Cash Flow Statement (Section 2(40)) Completely Exempt Financial statements consist only of Balance Sheet, P&L, and Notes.
Board Meetings (Section 173(5)) Zero BM if 1 Director If single director: no board meetings. If >1 director: min 1 BM per half-year (gap ≥ 90 days).
Annual Return (Section 92 / Rule 11) Form MGT-7A (Abridged) Signed solely by director. No mandatory PCS certification required.
Financial Statements Filing (Section 137) Form AOC-4 within 180 Days Filed within 180 days of FY close (September 27) instead of 30 days from AGM.
Commencement of Business (Section 10A) Form INC-20A within 180 Days Mandatory bank proof of capital deposit before commencing business.

6. Voluntary Conversion Architecture (Removal of Ceilings under Rule 6)

Historical Trap Eliminated: Prior to April 1, 2021, an OPC was forced to convert into a private company if its capital crossed ₹50 Lakhs or turnover crossed ₹2 Crores, and voluntary conversion was barred for 2 years.

Current Legal Position: Under substituted Rule 6 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014:

  • An OPC can have unlimited paid-up capital and turnover without being forced to convert.
  • An OPC can voluntarily convert into a Private Limited Company or Public Limited Company at any time without waiting periods.
  • Conversion requires:
    1. Altering MOA and AOA in accordance with Section 18.
    2. Increasing minimum members to 2 (for private) or 7 (for public).
    3. Increasing minimum directors to 2 (for private) or 3 (for public).
    4. Filing Form INC-6 with the ROC alongside audited financial statements.

7. Top 6 Practical & Commercial Pitfalls in OPC Operations

Based on corporate advisory practice, founders face six recurring statutory and commercial traps when operating an OPC:

1. Idle Cash Investment Bar (Rule 3(6))

Rule 3(6) prohibits an OPC from carrying out non-banking financial investment activities, including investment in securities of any bodies corporate. Parking surplus business profits into mutual funds, shares, or bonds violates Rule 3(6).

2. Inability to Raise Venture Capital / Issue ESOPs

An OPC cannot admit equity investors (angels, seed funds, VCs) or issue ESOPs to employees without undergoing formal statutory conversion into a multi-member Private Limited Company via Form INC-6.

3. Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) Prohibition under FEMA

While NRIs who are Indian citizens can incorporate an OPC, the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules, 2019 do not permit foreign investments (FDI) into OPCs.

4. Nominee Deadlock upon Founder Demise

If the nominee refuses to assume control, has relocated, or is incapacitated, corporate bank accounts and operations enter statutory freeze until legal transmission is resolved.

5. Personal Guarantees Render Limited Liability Illusory for Loans

Indian scheduled commercial banks routinely demand unconditional personal guarantees and personal asset collateral from the sole shareholder-director for credit facilities and loans.

6. Corporate Tax Double Taxation vs. LLP

An OPC pays corporate tax (22% + surcharge/cess under Section 115BAA). When profits are distributed as dividends, they are taxed again in the shareholder's hands at individual slab rates (up to 39%). In contrast, an LLP offers completely tax-free profit distributions under Section 10(2A).
