1. Nature, Legal Personality & Constitutional Association Rights

A Registered Society is a membership-based association formed by individuals or entities sharing common literary, scientific, charitable, artistic, educational, sports, or public welfare objectives, rooted in the fundamental freedom of association guaranteed under Article 19(1)(c) of the Constitution of India.

1. Corporate vs Quasi-Corporate Status

Unlike a Section 8 Company (which is a body corporate under the Companies Act 2013), a society under the 1860 Act has a distinct legal capacity to sue and be sued through its President/Secretary (Sec 6) and vests property in its Governing Body or Trustees (Sec 5) (*Board of Trustees, Ayurvedic & Unani Tibia College v. State of Delhi*, AIR 1962 SC 458).

2. Non-Distribution & Asset Lock

Surplus funds or profits cannot be distributed as dividends or bonuses among members. All income must be strictly applied toward the stated charitable or public utility objects in the Memorandum of Association.

3. Registration ≠ Tax Exemption

Registering with a State Registrar of Societies does NOT confer income-tax exemption or donor deductions. Separate applications in Form 10A/10AB with the Income Tax Department are mandatory for Sections 12AB and 80G.

2. Kerala Societies Registration Act, 2025 (Act 14 of 2025) — Verified Statutory Timeline

For decades, Kerala suffered a geographical legislative split: societies in the erstwhile Malabar region were registered under the central Societies Registration Act, 1860, whereas Travancore-Cochin societies were governed by the Travancore-Cochin Literary, Scientific and Charitable Societies Registration Act, 1955. Both legacy Acts have now been repealed and unified under the Kerala Societies Registration Act, 2025 (Act 14 of 2025).

Exact Gazette Chronology & Commencement Audit

  • Gazette Publication of Enactment: Published on November 7, 2025 via Law (Legislation-A) Department notification as Act 14 of 2025.
  • Official Commencement Date: Appointed as January 1, 2026 vide Notification G.O.(P) No. 206/2025/TAXES (S.R.O. No. 1435/2025) dated December 29, 2025.
  • Appointment of Registrars: Vide G.O.(P) No. 4/2026/TAXES (S.R.O. No. 33/2026) dated January 8, 2026, the Government appointed the District Registrar (General) in each district as the statutory Registrar under Section 2(j).
  • Deemed Validation & Transition: Compliant existing societies registered under the 1955 Act or the 1860 Act (Malabar) are deemed registered under Act 14 of 2025.

Section 4: Minimum Membership

Requires at least 7 members who have completed 18 years of age and are Indian citizens. Drops below 7 must be rectified within 6 months.

Section 18(3): Audit Mandate

Annual audit required by a practicing Chartered Accountant. Exemption: societies with annual income below ₹1,00,000 may have accounts audited by a non-governing-body member.

Section 19: 21-Day AGM Filing

Governing body list and audited statements must be filed within 21 days of each AGM. Five consecutive years of default triggers registration cancellation.

Subordinate Rules & Fee Transparency Note: While Act 14 of 2025 commenced on January 1, 2026, formal subordinate procedural rules and standardized fee/online portal formats remain subject to progressive notifications from the Department of Registration (registration.kerala.gov.in). Current administrative processing verifies district-level challan fees prior to filing.

3. State-Wise Legislative & Filing Matrix Across India

Society registration falls under Entry 32 of List II (State List) of the Seventh Schedule. Consequently, rules, forms, renewal obligations, and fee structures vary sharply across Indian states.

Kerala Kerala Societies Registration Act, 2025 (Act 14 of 2025) 7 (Indian Citizens ≥ 18 yrs) Within 21 days of AGM (Sec 19). CA audit required if income ≥ ₹1 Lakh. District Registrar (General) is authority.
Tamil Nadu Tamil Nadu Societies Registration Act, 1975 7 Within 6 months of AGM. Filing fees scaled ₹100–₹600 based on annual receipts/expenditure.
Karnataka Karnataka Societies Registration Act, 1960 7 (≥ 18 yrs) Within 14 days of AGM. Fee is ₹100 per ₹10 Lakhs (or part) of annual income/expenditure.

Delhi / Central Act States Societies Registration Act, 1860 (Central Act XXI of 1860) 7 (8 states for All-India Society) Annual list of managing body within 14 days of AGM under Section 4. Local verification required for district Registrar challans.

4. Memorandum of Association (MoA) & Bye-Laws Drafting Architecture

Registration requires two foundational documents signed by all founding members and certified by office bearers: the Memorandum of Association (MoA) (external charter) and the Rules and Regulations / Bye-Laws (internal governance charter).

Essential MoA Covenants

  • Name & Area of Operation: Must avoid prohibited Emblems & Names Act words or misleading government references.
  • Non-Profit & Non-Distribution Lock: Strict prohibition against paying profits or dividends to members (mandatory for 12AB tax exemption).
  • Dissolution Asset Lock: Upon dissolution (by 3/5th majority under Sec 13), net surplus assets CANNOT revert to members—must transfer to another registered society with similar objects.

Mandatory Bye-Laws Clauses

  • Membership & Expulsion Rules: Admission fees, subscriptions, voting eligibility, and natural justice procedures before termination.
  • AGM / SGM Quorum & Notice: Minimum 14–21 days clear notice, quorum limits, and democratic election of Governing Body (President, Secretary, Treasurer).
  • Banking & Audit Controls: Dual signatory mandates (typically Treasurer + President/Secretary) and mandatory annual audit.

5. Post-Registration Compliance: PAN, 12AB/80G, NGO Darpan & FCRA

Securing a Society Registration Certificate is merely Step 1. Receiving grants, tax-deductible donations, CSR funds, or foreign contributions requires distinct statutory approvals:

1. Entity PAN, TAN & Bank Account

Apply for society PAN in Form 49A (status: AOP/Trust) and TAN if deducting TDS. Bank current accounts require the Registration Certificate, certified MoA/Bye-laws, PAN, and Governing Body resolution designating signatories.

2. Income Tax 12AB & 80G (Form 10A/10AB)

New societies apply for 3-year provisional registration in Form 10A (under 12AB for income exemption and 80G for donor tax deductions), later converted to 5-year regular status via Form 10AB. Transitional continuity applies under the Income-tax Act, 2025 effective April 1, 2026.

3. NITI Aayog NGO Darpan & CSR-1

NGO Darpan issues a Unique ID required before applying for Central/State government grants or FCRA. Accessing corporate CSR funds requires filing MCA Form CSR-1 (requires active 12AB/80G and 3-year track record if not promoted by the corporate).

4. FCRA 2010 / 2020 Foreign Funding

Receiving foreign donations without MHA FCRA Registration (3 years activity + ₹15 Lakh spend) or Prior Permission is a criminal offence. All foreign funds must arrive exclusively via a designated SBI Main Branch, New Delhi FCRA account; sub-granting is prohibited and admin costs are capped at 20%.

6. Annual Returns, Amendments, Disputes & Dissolution

Active governance requires maintaining statutory Minutes Books, Membership Registers, Asset Registers, and timely statutory submissions.

  • Amending MoA or Objectives (Sec 12 Central Act / State equivalents): Requires convening a Special General Meeting with statutory prior notice and approval by a 3/5th majority of members present and voting, ratified at a second confirming meeting or filed with the Registrar.
  • Voluntary Dissolution (Sec 13 Central Act): Requires a resolution passed by not less than 3/5th of total members; any dispute over asset distribution is referred to the principal Civil Court of original jurisdiction.
  • Misappropriation & Removal: Office bearers hold funds in a fiduciary capacity; embezzlement attracts civil recovery plus criminal breach of trust prosecution under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS).
